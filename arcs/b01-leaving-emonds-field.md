The first ten chapters of *The Eye of the World* are one long leavetaking. They begin with Rand walking a muddy road the day before a festival, sure that "no one ever leaves the Two Rivers", and end with him riding north at night beside an Aes Sedai, a Warder, a gleeman and three friends. Everything between is the slow removal of each reason to stay.

- **The method.** One point of view, Rand's; one place, Emond's Field and its farms; and a series of strangers, each bringing a piece of the outside world.
- **The turn.** Winternight (Ch. 5) is the hinge: before it, signs the village ignores; after it, facts no one can deny.
- **The cost.** By Chapter 10 Rand has lost his home, the farm, his certainty about his birth, and his father's company. He keeps the sword and the promise to come back.
- **The party.** Seven ride out. The Wisdom, Nynaeve, is not among them, and the text gives no sign yet that she will follow.

## Stages

The ten chapters fall into five movements of two chapters each. Every movement takes one thing from Rand and gives him one thing he did not ask for.

| Movement | Chapters | What happens | What Rand loses | What he gains |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Signs | 1 An Empty Road, 2 Strangers | A rider whose cloak the wind ignores; a raven; a lady, her swordsman, a peddler | The belief that nothing ever happens here | A silver coin, "a token" of a bond he does not understand |
| 2. The world arrives | 3 The Peddler, 4 The Gleeman | War in Ghealdan, a false Dragon, Aes Sedai riding south; Thom's tales; Egwene's braid | The Egwene of his childhood; "No one ever leaves the Two Rivers" starts to sound like a wish | Tam believes him about the rider: "that others knew and believed made all the difference" |
| 3. Winternight | 5 Winternight, 6 The Westwood | Trollocs break into the farm; Tam is wounded; Rand kills Narg; the night walk and the fever talk | The farm, and the certainty of his birth: "Light, who am I?" | His first courage, and Tam's heron-mark sword |
| 4. The burned village | 7 Out of the Woods, 8 A Place of Safety | Emond's Field half in ashes; Wisdom and Mayor fail; Moiraine heals Tam and lays out the hunt | Home as a place of safety; the right to stay | Tam's life; a destination, Tar Valon; a debt to an Aes Sedai |
| 5. Leavetaking | 9 Tellings of the Wheel, 10 Leavetaking | A dream of Shayol Ghul and a white tower; Tam's blessing; the tale of Manetheren; the night ride | His father's company, and the village itself | A party of seven, and his own promise: "I will come back" |

**The hinge.** Chapters 1 to 4 pile up signs the village cannot read. Chapter 5 turns every one of them into fact. Chapters 6 to 10 spend the consequences.

## Rand across ten chapters

Rand changes less than his world does. He ends Chapter 10 still a shepherd at heart, but every belief he started with has been tested, and most have broken.

| Aspect | Chapter 1 | Chapter 10 | Turning point |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What he believes about stories | Gleemen's tales; the Dragon who saves mankind is a fool's tale | "All the stories are real"; Trollocs, Fades, Aes Sedai, Manetheren | Narg at the door (Ch. 5); Haral's "So it seems, lad" (Ch. 7) |
| Who he is | Tam's son, a Westwood shepherd, a head taller than anyone | Still "my father", but the fever talk sits unasked between them | Tam's baby in the snow (Ch. 6); the unasked question (Ch. 9) |
| What he wants | Bel Tine, the archery, Egwene without confusion | Tam safe, Egwene safe, and to come back | Tam's wound (Ch. 5); Egwene joining the party (Ch. 10) |
| What he fears | The black rider's hatred | The Shadow inside his dreams: "Serve me" | The Myrddraal "want talk you" (Ch. 5); the dream (Ch. 9) |
| How he sees Aes Sedai | The villains of every story | A woman who healed his father and whose price is still unnamed | "Any price in my power" (Ch. 7); Tam's "take care around her" (Ch. 9) |
| How he acts | Holds back; cannot hold the flame and the void | Goes back alone into a wrecked house; resists the dream with anger; chooses to leave | Killing Narg (Ch. 5); "That's why I have to leave, isn't it?" (Ch. 8) |
| What he carries | A bow and a quiver | Tam's heron-mark sword | Tam: "Keep it" (Ch. 9) |

**The pattern of his courage.** Rand is brave only when someone else is at stake: Tam on Winternight, the village in Chapter 8, Egwene in Chapter 10. For himself he mostly wants to go home. That is the trait the series will test hardest.

## Emond's Field

The village is the second protagonist of these chapters. It starts as a comic chorus arguing about omens, is burned on Winternight, and ends with a past it had forgotten and patrols in attic armour.

| Aspect | Before Winternight (Ch. 1–4) | After Winternight (Ch. 7–10) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mood | Festival preparations, gossip, a winter that won't end | Ashes, rebuilding, lit windows with watchers inside |
| How it meets the outside | Half-remembered stories, a closed inn door, a shouting match over Fain's news | A mob by the peddler's burned wagon, then shame |
| Its leaders | The Council and the Women's Circle quarrel; Bran is shrewd and good-humoured | Nynaeve can do nothing for Tam; Bran and Haral stand beside a stranger |
| What it believes about Aes Sedai | Villains who broke the world; Bili says they don't exist | One healed half the wounded; some still want her gone |
| What it knows of itself | "Two Rivers folk": stubborn farmers | The heirs of Manetheren, who died on this ground as "Aemon's Field" |
| How it defends itself | A watch organized by the Council (Ch. 4) | Jon Thane's patrols, "stepping to three different drums" |
| What it loses | (nothing yet) | Half its houses, the Spring Pole, Fain, and four of its young people in one night |

**What the village never learns.** The Trollocs came for three of its boys. Only the Mayor's questions come close, and Rand keeps the answer from him.

### The missing eighth

Nynaeve is on stage from Chapter 3 and drives Chapters 4 and 7: the Wisdom who quarrels with the Council and cannot save Tam. In Chapter 10 she is simply absent. No one mentions her, and nothing in the stable points to her. The reader of a first reading has no reason to expect her; the reader who knows the book sees the gap.

| What the chapters show of Nynaeve | Why her absence matters |
| --- | --- |
| A Wisdom who "listens to the wind" and takes Egwene as apprentice (Ch. 3) | Egwene leaves without her teacher's leave |
| Defies the Council (Ch. 4) | She is not a woman to let others decide for her |
| "There's nothing I can do" for Tam, then "Our troubles are just beginning" (Ch. 7) | She has seen the Shadow's work and her own limits |
| Not consulted, not told, not seen (Ch. 8–10) | The secret leaving cuts her out, and she is responsible for the people taken |

## What the reader knows that Rand doesn't

By Chapter 10 the reader is ahead of Rand on almost everything that matters, and the opening chapters are built so that the gap keeps growing.

| What the reader holds | Where it came from | Rand's view at the leavetaking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A man who could channel destroyed his own family, and a mountain rose where he died | The prologue, before Chapter 1 | Has never heard of Lews Therin; saw a split mountain in a dream |
| The Dragon is reborn, and a false Dragon is loose | The prologue; Fain's news (Ch. 3) | A fool's tale, and news from Ghealdan |
| Tam found a baby on the slopes of a mountain during a war | Tam's fever (Ch. 6) | Heard it, refused it, did not ask |
| Moiraine is looking for one young man of a certain age | Her questions (Ch. 2); her case (Ch. 8) | Believes the Trollocs want "us", all three |
| The Dark One is reaching for Rand in his dreams | The dream (Ch. 9) | Blames hunger and a sword hilt in his ribs |
| Aes Sedai do not lie, but their truth is "not always the truth you think" | Tam (Ch. 9); Thom (Ch. 7) | Has the warning, not yet the experience |
| Thom knows far more than a gleeman should | His wariness (Ch. 4, 7); the Draghkar (Ch. 10) | A showman along for the ride |
| The party is not complete | Nynaeve's absence (Ch. 10), which only a second reading notices | Seven, and nothing to add |

**Why this works.** The reader is never ahead by guessing, only by having been shown. Every row but the last is on the page, and even the last is a gap the page leaves; Rand simply cannot afford to look at it.

## Craft lessons

The lessons the ten chapter cards share, stated once.

1. **Make the reader love the home before you burn it.** Four chapters of festival, gossip and chores make Winternight a loss, not a spectacle.
2. **Bring the world in before you send the hero out.** Strangers, news and tales arrive one by one, so the outside is familiar by the time Rand has to face it.
3. **Plant signs the characters cannot read.** The rider, the raven, the coins and Moiraine's questions are clear to the reader and meaningless to the village. The gap is the suspense.
4. **Turn every story into fact, one at a time.** Trollocs, Fades, Aes Sedai and Manetheren each move from tale to truth, so the reader learns to take every legend seriously.
5. **Remove the helpers in order.** Smith, Wisdom, Mayor, father: each fails a little more, until only the feared stranger is left.
6. **Let the hero choose what he is forced to do.** Moiraine never orders Rand to go; he says it himself. A forced choice still has to be spoken by the one who makes it.
7. **Assemble the fellowship from people already met.** No traveller in the stable is new. The party feels inevitable because every member was introduced chapters earlier.
8. **Leave a gap you will fill.** Nynaeve's absence is invisible on a first reading, and fair when she returns.
9. **End the opening on motion.** Ten chapters of walking, waiting and talking end at a gallop. The book's first movement closes as its second begins.
