# WoT Thread — The Pattern and Ta'veren

Up: [Wheel of Time — Story Map](../story-map.md) · Sister thread: [WoT Thread — The Shadow](shadow.md) · Ta'veren: [WoT Character — Rand](../characters/rand.md) · [WoT Character — Mat](../characters/mat.md) · [WoT Character — Perrin](../characters/perrin.md) · Ages and legends: [WoT — Tales and Legends](../world/tales-and-legends.md)

The Pattern is the series' answer to fate, and its question is how much room it leaves for choice. The Wheel of Time weaves every life into a Pattern; a few people, the ta'veren, are woven so tightly that the Pattern bends around them. In the first seven chapters the idea is only a phrase that Moiraine, Thom and a blacksmith repeat, while the plot quietly behaves as if it were true: three boys, three sightings, three coins, and every stranger arriving at once.

## How the Pattern works

The cosmology is simple to state and slow to reveal. Book 1 gives only its surface; the rest comes out over the series.

- **The Wheel.** Time is a wheel with seven spokes, the Ages. Each Age comes again, so the past is also the future: "Ages that were and will be" (Thom, Ch. 4). Our own world sits somewhere on the Wheel as a forgotten Age; Thom's tales of Lenn and Mosk are its legends.
- **The Pattern.** The Wheel weaves lives as threads into the Pattern of an Age, and Ages into the Great Pattern. "No one knows the Great Pattern" (Moiraine, Ch. 2).
- **Souls reborn.** Threads are spun out again in later Ages: "Men wear many names, many faces. Different faces, but always the same man" (Ch. 2). The Dragon is the great example.
- **Ta'veren.** A few threads are central: the Pattern bends the lives around them, drawing people and chance toward them. The word does not appear in Chapters 1–7.
- **What it is not.** The Pattern is not the Dark One's. His aim is to break the Wheel itself, which is why the Last Battle matters on a scale beyond one Age.
- **The open question.** If the Pattern weaves everything, are choices real? The series keeps answering "both": the Pattern sets the threads; people choose how they hold them.

## The three ta'veren

The Shadow and the Tower hunt one young man; the Pattern has woven three. Each one's pull takes a different shape, and each resists it in his own way. All of this is from memory of later books.

| Ta'veren | How the pull shows | What he resists | What it costs him |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rand | The strongest: chance goes wild around him (sudden weddings, freak accidents), nations bend to him, prophecies fulfil themselves | Being only the Dragon, a tool of prophecy | His ordinary life, and nearly his humanity |
| Mat | Luck: dice, cards, arrows that miss; battles and destinies find him wherever he runs | Duty, ties, anyone steering him | His freedom, which the Pattern keeps taking back |
| Perrin | People choose to follow him; his camps gather nobles, refugees and wolves | Leadership, and the violence it asks of him | His wish to stay a blacksmith |

**Why three.** One ta'veren would be a chosen one; three make a weave. Their pulls tug at each other: Rand drags Mat and Perrin back to him, which the books show as colours swirling in their minds whenever they think of him.

## Book by book

The thread runs from a saying to a cosmology to a choice. Its answer comes at the very end, and it is about free will.

| Books | What the thread shows | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The Wheel as a saying; the word ta'veren arrives with an Ogier and an Aes Sedai; coincidences gather around the boys | The reader learns to read luck as design |
| 2 | Three ta'veren in one village; the Horn of Valere calls back heroes whose souls the Wheel spins out again; Rand glimpses other lives he might have lived | Rebirth is literal, and other lives were possible |
| 3–4 | Prophecies fulfil themselves around Rand; in Rhuidean he sees the Aiel's past turn after turn | The Pattern repeats, but with variations |
| 5–6 | Chance runs wild near Rand: sudden marriages, freak accidents | Being central is a disturbance, not a blessing |
| 7–11 | Each ta'veren draws his own crowd: Perrin's camp, Mat's band and his foretold marriage | Three weaves, pulling apart and back together |
| 12–13 | On Dragonmount Rand sees that lives come again, so love and chances come again too | Rebirth turns from a burden into a reason to choose |
| 14 | At Shayol Ghul Rand refuses a world where people could not choose evil, and keeps the Wheel turning | The series' answer: the Pattern sets the threads, but choice is what makes them human |

## Craft lessons

1. **State the cosmology as a proverb first.** "The Wheel weaves as the Wheel wills" is small talk before it is metaphysics, so the reader absorbs the rule without a lecture.
2. **Let coincidence be evidence.** Three sightings and three coins look like plot convenience until the world names the force behind them; the device turns a weakness into a mystery.
3. **Make fate felt, not only said.** Ta'veren effects are shown as odd events around the hero, so the theme becomes something the reader can watch.
4. **Give one idea several shapes.** Rand's pull bends nations, Mat's bends dice, Perrin's bends people: one rule, three characters.
5. **Answer the big question with a choice.** The series settles fate against free will in an act, Rand's refusal, not in an explanation.
