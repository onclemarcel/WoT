# WoT — Tales and Legends

Oct 12, 2026 · @OncleMarcel

Up: [WoT — World Index](index.md) · First told: [EotW Ch. 4 — The Gleeman](../chapters/b01-eye-of-the-world/ch04-the-gleeman.md) · Teller: [WoT Character — Thom](../characters/thom.md)

This card collects the tales, legends and half-remembered histories that people tell inside the story, and grows as the chapters add more. The best one-sentence history of the world comes from Thom in Chapter 4:

> "I will tell you of the end of the Age of Legends, of the Dragon, and his attempt to free the Dark One into the world of men. I will tell of the Time of Madness, when Aes Sedai shattered the world; of the Trolloc Wars, when men battled Trollocs for rule of the earth; of the War of the Hundred Years, when men battled men and the nations of our day were wrought."

It is a summary, and like most tales in the series it is half true. The order of the ages is right; who did what is often wrong.

| Today | Ghealdan at war, false Dragons, Aes Sedai riding south | The Last Battle approaches; the Dragon is reborn |

## How Jordan built a world of stories

Jordan made the world feel old by giving it more stories than the plot needs. He did it with a few repeatable moves.

1. **Borrow from everywhere.** He drew on Arthurian legend, Norse and Hindu myth, and real history, then let time wear the names down: Arthur Pendragon becomes Artur Paendrag, Moscow becomes Mosk.
2. **Make time a wheel.** Because ages repeat, our world can sit in the far past or far future of his, and its heroes can survive as garbled legends. The cycle is the series' title.
3. **Name far more than you explain.** Most tales in Thom's list never get told. A bare title like "King Darith and the Fall of the House of" implies a history without spending pages on it.
4. **Let some tales come true.** The Green Man, Jain Farstrider and the Dragon are all "just stories" in Chapter 4, and all turn out to be real. The reader learns to take every legend seriously.
5. **Let tellers get it wrong.** The Two Rivers' version of the Breaking blames the wrong people, which makes history a mystery the characters must solve.
