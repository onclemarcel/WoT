# WoT — Aes Sedai

Oct 16, 2026 · @OncleMarcel

Up: [WoT — World Index](index.md) · Seat: [WoT — Tar Valon and the White Tower](tar-valon.md) · On stage: [WoT Character — Moiraine](../characters/moiraine.md) · Chapter cards: [EotW Ch. 2 — Strangers](../chapters/b01-eye-of-the-world/ch02-strangers.md) · [EotW Ch. 3 — The Peddler](../chapters/b01-eye-of-the-world/ch03-the-peddler.md)

Aes Sedai are the women who wield the One Power, and in the first six chapters they are a rumour before they are a fact. The village knows them only as the people who "broke the world", while one of them sits at the Winespring Inn handing out coins. The gap between that rumour and the woman on stage is how the series introduces them.

- **What the chapters show (Ch. 2–12).** Moiraine's ageless face, her Great Serpent ring and a coin bearing the Flame of Tar Valon (Ch. 2); the word "Aes Sedai" spoken for the first time in Fain's news, with a party riding south from Tar Valon (Ch. 3); Thom's "I make a point of never knowing anything about Aes Sedai" (Ch. 4); "no wars or Aes Sedai" by Tam's fire (Ch. 5); Bran names Moiraine an Aes Sedai and Lan a Warder, after she called ball lightning down on the Trollocs, tired out "even with an angreal"; Rand asks her to heal Tam and offers "any price" (Ch. 7); she heals him with an ivory angreal the Amyrlin almost refused her, says Healing needs "will and strength" in the patient and that much was lost since the Age of Legends, and would have needed "half a dozen of my sisters" to stop the raid (Ch. 8). On the road north she washes the fatigue from horses and riders but cannot do it for herself, will not try to kill a Draghkar from a galloping horse, and raises a fog to hide the party (Ch. 11). She sinks the ferry with a whirlpool ("an unfortunate occurrence"), says not ten women in Tar Valon could have laid that fog unaided, and gives Egwene her first lesson: the True Source and its two halves, saidin tainted by the Dark One, men who channel dying unless the Red Ajah gentles them, untaught women becoming Wisdoms, and the Source that is never used up (Ch. 12).
- **What the village believes.** They caused the Breaking; some stories call them Darkfriends; Bili Congar says they don't exist (Ch. 3).
- **What is true.** They exist and cannot speak a lie. It was the male Aes Sedai, driven mad by the taint on saidin, who broke the world; the women held back what they could.
- **How they work.** Each belongs to one of seven Ajahs (Moiraine is Blue), swears Three Oaths, and may bond Warders, as Moiraine has bonded Lan.
- **Seat.** The White Tower in Tar Valon, ruled by the Amyrlin Seat; see the Tar Valon card.

## Aes Sedai across the series

The arc of the Aes Sedai runs from a feared legend to a broken institution to a rebuilt one. Each book moves them between guide, rival and jailer of the Dragon.

| Book | Where the Aes Sedai stand | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | One Aes Sedai, Moiraine, finds the boys and leads them out; Egwene and Nynaeve learn they can channel | The rumour becomes a guide, and two village women take the first step toward the Tower |
| 2 | The Amyrlin comes north; the Black Ajah, Aes Sedai who serve the Shadow, is revealed | The order is not one thing: some of it belongs to the enemy |
| 3 | Egwene, Nynaeve and Elayne hunt the Black Ajah | The young women act on their own, outside the Tower's control |
| 4 | The Tower splits: the Amyrlin is deposed | The institution breaks over how to deal with the Dragon |
| 5–6 | The rebels raise Egwene as their Amyrlin; at Dumai's Wells Tower sisters cage Rand, and some then swear fealty to him | Aes Sedai become his jailers, then reluctant servants |
| 7–11 | Two Towers face each other; the rebels besiege Tar Valon; Egwene is taken inside | A long stalemate inside one order, mirroring the world's |
| 12 | The Black Ajah is purged; the Tower reunites under Egwene | The order heals by facing what it hid |
| 13–14 | The Tower fights in the Last Battle | The legend becomes an army |

**The constant.** Through every change, Aes Sedai speak no lie and tell little of the truth. The craft question the order poses is how much a reader can trust a character who never lies.
