# WoT — Taren Ferry

Oct 20, 2026 · @OncleMarcel

Up: [WoT — World Index](index.md) · Region: [WoT — The Two Rivers](two-rivers.md) · Chapter cards: [EotW Ch. 11 — The Road to Taren Ferry](../chapters/b01-eye-of-the-world/ch11-the-road-to-taren-ferry.md) · [EotW Ch. 12 — Across the Taren](../chapters/b01-eye-of-the-world/ch12-across-the-taren.md)

Taren Ferry is the Two Rivers' only door, and the first place the heroes see where people are not like them. For ten chapters it is a name on the edge of the map. In Chapters 11 and 12 the party reaches it in fog, buys a night crossing with gold, and leaves the ferry in pieces behind them.

- **Where:** the northernmost Two Rivers village, on the south bank of the Taren, at the end of the North Road from Emond's Field and Watch Hill. Beyond the river lies the road to Baerlon.
- **What it looks like:** tall houses on high redstone foundations, built against the spring flood when the melt from the Mountains of Mist swells the Taren (Ch. 11).
- **The crossing:** a plank landing like "a bridge that led nowhere", a high-sided barge with a ramp at each end, and wrist-thick guide-ropes across the river. Six haulers walk the ropes to pull it over (Ch. 12).
- **The river:** wide, deep and fast, with currents said to pull under the strongest swimmer. The boys find it "not half as wide" as they had heard (Ch. 12).
- **The people:** they look down on "the lower villages", carry odd names like Hilltop and Stoneboat, and are known for slyness: shake hands with one and count your fingers after (Ch. 11).
- **Its one named citizen:** Master Hightower, the narrow-faced ferryman, greedy and frightened in turn.

The village grows from a word into a place, then into a trap the party escapes. Each mention moves it one step closer.

## Across the series

After Chapter 12 the village mostly drops out of the story. It matters again only when the Two Rivers does.

| Book | Where Taren Ferry stands | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | The heroes' exit; its ferry is destroyed | The last of home, and the first of the world |
| 4 | Back in view when Perrin returns to defend the Two Rivers; recalled only loosely here | The door now lets enemies in as well as out |
| 5–14 | Rarely seen; the Two Rivers grows and joins the wider world | The edge of the map stops being the edge |

**Craft lesson.** A border town is most useful as contrast. Taren Ferry's slyness makes the reader feel how sheltered Emond's Field was, before the party meets a world that is truly strange.
