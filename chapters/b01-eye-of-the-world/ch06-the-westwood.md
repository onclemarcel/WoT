Chapter 6 is a night walk that ends with Rand no longer sure who he is. He drags his feverish father through the Westwood toward Emond's Field and hides by the Quarry Road while the black rider leads twenty Trollocs past. Through it all Tam talks in his fever, about a war, a tree, and a baby found in the snow, and the last words change the story's question: "Light, who am I?"

## Setting

The chapter has one setting, the dark woods between the farm and the village, and one line through it: the Quarry Road, running west to the farm and east to Emond's Field. Rand keeps to the trees, close enough to the road to find his way and close enough to be seen.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Westwood at night | Roots, brambles, dips and rises; branches that shift and threaten in the wind | Every sound might be a Trolloc; Rand stops at each one |
| The Quarry Road | The only sure way to the village; Rand turns back the moment he finds himself on it | The route the enemy will use: the rider and his column pass "not twenty paces away" |
| The edge of the trees beside the road | Cover, barely: "awfully bare" in moonlight | The spot where the rider stops and turns his hood toward Rand |
| Emond's Field, imagined | Lit windows, fiddles playing "Jaem's Folly" and "Heron on the Wing", Haral Luhhan singing, Mat's pranks | The safe place Rand walks toward; the reader has reason to doubt it is still safe |
| Tam's battlefields, in fever | The Dragonwall, the field at Marath, Cairhien burning, the Shining Walls, a snowy mountain slope | A war in the wide world, and the place where Rand was found |

**Two directions.** Rand moves east, toward the village. The rider and his Trollocs come from the east, the village side, and run west toward the farm. Rand never asks where they have been, and the reader should.

## Characters

Rand carries the chapter alone; Tam speaks only in fever, and the black rider never speaks at all. Each is defined by what he cannot control: Rand his fear, Tam his memory, the Trollocs their master.

| Character | Mindset | Wants | Key moment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rand | Afraid, exhausted, hungry, angry at himself for each mistake; keeps going because he must | To get Tam to Nynaeve; later, only for it all to end | Hears that he was a baby found on a battlefield, and refuses to listen: "You are my father" |
| Tam | Delirious, sometimes strong and angry, mostly murmuring | In his dream, to be heard: by Kari, by "the fools" who underrated an enemy | Tells the story of finding a crying child in the snow, and naming him Rand |
| The black rider | Silent, searching, full of hatred "for everything that lived" | To find whoever escaped the farm | Stops exactly opposite Rand, turns his hood, then gallops west |
| The Trollocs | Disciplined now: a double file, every foot striking at once | To follow the rider | Twenty run past in step, "as if obeying a single mind" |

**Named but not on stage:** Kari, Rand's mother, dead fifteen years; Mistress al'Vere and her honeycakes; Haral Luhhan and his wife; Cenn Buie; Mat, whose pranks Rand pictures; Laman, whose "sin" and "pride" Tam blames; the King of Cairhien; the Green Man; Ogier and Aiel, as stories that may be true.

## Tam's fever talk

Tam's ravings are the chapter's real plot. They come in pieces, out of order, and Rand half listens, but together they tell one story: a war against a people who "make no peace with strangers", and a baby found at the end of it. The text never names that people; every clue points to the Aiel.

| What Tam says | What Rand hears | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "You're still lovely, Kari" | His mother, dead fifteen years, alive in Tam's mind | The fever has taken Tam back to his life with his wife |
| "They came over the Dragonwall like a flood"; "Laman's sin" | A nightmare about invaders | The Aiel War: the Aiel crossed the mountains of the Dragonwall to punish King Laman of Cairhien |
| "They called them savages... Before the nations stood together" | A soldier's bitterness | The nations underrated the Aiel and lost battle after battle before they allied |
| The field at Marath; the towers of Cairhien burning; the Shining Walls | Names Rand does not know | Real battles of that war; the Shining Walls are Tar Valon's, where the Aiel were turned back |
| A sapling of Avendesora, a gift to the King, cut down after a hundred years: "Blood the price for Avendoraldera" | A muddled Green Man story | The Aiel gave Cairhien a cutting of the Tree of Life as a sign of peace; Laman cut it down, and the war followed |
| "Their women fight alongside the men" | A strange detail | Aiel women warriors, the Maidens of the Spear |
| A woman gave birth alone on a mountain slope and died; a baby blue with cold, crying in the snow | A fever-dream | The truth: Tam found Rand on the battlefield, born to one of the enemy's women |
| "Rand is a good name" | His own name, in the dream | Tam and Kari named the foundling and raised him as their son |

**Why it is told this way.** A plain confession would settle the question. Fever talk leaves Rand, and the reader, free to doubt it: "It was just a fever-dream."

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter connects clues the earlier chapters only laid out. Rand's grey eyes, his height and "as tall as an Aielman" now meet a baby found among Aiel warriors.

| Sign | What the village sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A baby found on "the slope of the mountain" after a battle | (the village never hears it) | The slope of Dragonmount, near Tar Valon, at the end of the Aiel War: the Dragon's prophesied birthplace |
| The mother was one of "their women" who fight | (unknown) | Rand's birth mother was an Aiel Maiden of the Spear; his height and grey eyes come from her people |
| Rand's grey eyes, his height, Thom's "as tall as an Aielman" | Oddities | Plain evidence of Aiel blood, now matched to Tam's story |
| The rider's cloak hangs still in the wind; his horse goes silent at will | Uncanny | A Myrddraal: the black rider is the Fade Narg feared |
| Trollocs in perfect step, "as if obeying a single mind" | Discipline | A Fade binds Trollocs to its will; without one they are a rabble |
| The column comes from the east, the village side | (Rand does not ask) | The village has likely been attacked too; Rand is walking toward it |
| The rider stops opposite Rand and turns his hood | A near miss | The Fade can sense more than it sees; it hunts by feel as much as sight |
| Rand feels the gaze "like grit scraping his bones" | Fear | Rand senses the Shadow, an early sign that he is more than he seems |
| "Next he might actually meet the Green Man, or an Ogier... or a wild, black-veiled Aielman" | A tired joke | He will meet all three in Book 1 and the books after |

## Craft lessons

1. **Reveal a secret through an unreliable voice.** Fever lets Tam say everything while letting Rand, and the reader, doubt it.
2. **Scatter the pieces out of order.** War, tree, baby, name: the reader assembles the story a beat before Rand does, and feels the blow when he does.
3. **Put exposition inside suspense.** The world's recent history arrives while a Fade and twenty Trollocs pass twenty paces away.
4. **Make the body carry the scene.** Hunger, the groove of the blanket strap, cold sweat and stumbles keep a long walk physical and real.
5. **Let the hero picture home.** Rand's daydream of fiddles and Haral Luhhan's singing gives him something to walk toward, and the reader something to fear for.
6. **Show a villain by what he does not do.** The rider never speaks or attacks. A still cloak, a silent horse and a turned hood are enough.
7. **End on a question, not an event.** "Light, who am I?" closes the chapter on identity, not danger, and changes what the story is about.
