Chapter 13 is a quiet week on the road, and its subject is who chooses. Lan starts teaching the boys their weapons, and Moiraine teaches Egwene in private. Egwene unbraids her hair, and Rand quarrels with her about Aes Sedai and Darkfriends. When Mat dreams aloud of Illian, Moiraine says she would destroy them herself before letting the Dark One have them. Rand eavesdrops and learns there is a second woman in Emond's Field who can channel. At Baerlon's gate the news is bad: Whitecloaks in the town, a battle in Ghealdan, and Aes Sedai dead. Moiraine tells the boys they have "no choice", after she has made every choice for them.

## Setting

The chapter moves from empty country to the first town, and the land keeps saying the same thing: it is not as different from home as the boys expect, until suddenly it is.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The tree cave at dawn | Moiraine laying hands on each traveller; every trace of the camp cleared before the sun is high | Her own exhaustion, which no one can lift |
| The forest north of the Taren | Late winter: bare branches, old snow under the evergreens, nettles and stinkweed, few birds | No pursuit at all, which slowly lulls the boys |
| The North Road and its detours | A road that may have another name here; the party snakes through the woods and circles every farm | How far from people Rand has come: farther than at the foot of the Mountains of Mist |
| The first farm | A frame house, a tan barn, boys with sheep, a tabac shed: "no different from back home" | The boys' wish that the world beyond the Taren be strange |
| The night camps | Small, hidden fires; flatbread, cheese and dried meat; Thom's stories with happy endings | Moiraine and Egwene's private talks in the dark |
| Baerlon from the hill | A twenty-foot log wall with watchtowers, slate and tile roofs, hundreds of chimneys, wagons on two roads | Whitecloaks inside, and perhaps Darkfriends |
| The gate, the warehouse streets, the Stag and Lion | A side gate opened for "Mistress Alys"; a hidden latch in a fence; an inn four storeys high | Moiraine and Lan have been here before, under other names |

## Lessons on the road, news at the gate

The road to Baerlon becomes a school. Every adult in the party teaches someone, and each lesson shows what the teacher values.

| Teacher | Pupil | Lesson | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lan | Mat, Perrin, Rand | The bow first, then axe and sword: block, parry, strike, "almost a dance" | He starts the lessons himself; Moiraine watches, and the next evening tells him to continue |
| Lan | Rand | "Blank your mind... Empty it of hate or fear" | Tam's flame and the void is a fighter's discipline; Lan gives Rand an "unreadable look" when he names it |
| Thom | Mat (and the others) | Juggling; Mat reaches four stones | The gleeman passes on his craft, as he passes on stories |
| Thom | Rand | The Stone of Tear and the Karaethon Cycle | He answers, but wishes the Stone to stand "till I am dust" |
| Moiraine | Egwene | Nightly private talks: the Five Powers, the men who broke the world, what the Tower is really like | Aes Sedai are "human", brave and cowardly, kind and cruel; the Power will not change who she is |
| Moiraine | Rand, Mat, Perrin | "Before I let the Dark One have you, I will destroy you myself" | The lesson in what she is willing to do |

**The news at the gate.** Avin's gossip is the first report from the wider world since Fain's in Chapter 3, and it is all worse.

| Item | What Avin says | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whitecloaks in Baerlon | Arrived the day Moiraine and Lan left; the Governor lets only about ten inside; the rest camp north of town | An order that hates Aes Sedai is waiting where Moiraine must rest |
| The Dragon's Fang | Already on some doors in Baerlon | The accusation from Emond's Field is a habit of the wider world |
| Ghealdan | A big battle; the Aes Sedai who went there may all be dead | Fain's news of Chapter 3 has turned into a war |
| The false Dragon | Can use the One Power, "thousands following him"; moving east and south toward Tear; calls them the People of the Dragon | His title echoes a prophecy about the Stone of Tear |
| Saldaea | "Some kind of trouble up there" | The world's trouble is not only in the south |

**Thom's prophecy.** The Stone of Tear will not fall until the People of the Dragon come to it, and not until the Sword That Cannot Be Touched is wielded by the Dragon's hand. Rand spots the paradox: the sword lies inside the Stone. Thom: "A prophecy that was easily fulfilled would not be worth much."

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter plants its biggest clue in a sentence Moiraine wishes she had not said, and three prophecies inside a gleeman's grumbling.

| Sign | What the party sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Surely never before two in one village"; "Her road lies another way, I fear" | A slip Moiraine orders Egwene to forget | The second woman is Nynaeve, the Wisdom who channels without knowing it. Egwene guesses Kari Thane and Lara Ayellan |
| Moiraine looks straight at Rand's hiding place, then studies the sleepers | Bad luck for an eavesdropper | Often read as Moiraine knowing exactly who listened; the text never says |
| Lan's "unreadable look" when Rand names the flame and the void | Nothing | The void is a blademaster's discipline; Tam knew more of the sword than a shepherd should |
| Rand's three arrows almost touching, shot from the void | A good archer | The void will later be Rand's way to the Source |
| "Illian isn't full of Aes Se-" | Mat's joke cut off | Mat's lasting distrust of Aes Sedai, and of being steered |
| The Great Hunt of the Horn, in Thom's repertoire | A happy-ending tale | The next book's title, and a hunt the heroes will join |
| The Sword That Cannot Be Touched, in the Heart of the Stone | A riddle | Callandor; Rand will draw it, and the Stone will fall |
| "The People of the Dragon" | The false Dragon's name for his army | The prophecy means another people entirely: the Aiel |
| The false Dragon who can channel, marching toward Tear | Distant war | Logain, whose fate will cross the heroes' road |
| Rand's dream: every man claims to be the Dragon, every woman wears Moiraine's blue stone | A nightmare | His two fears, the Dragon and the Aes Sedai, are already one knot |
| Avin: "You give me plenty when you went out"; he let Thom through "some days back" | Gossip | Moiraine and Lan prepared this road on the way south; Thom came through Baerlon on his own |
| "The weather all crazy", wolves howling at the walls | Small talk | The winter that will not end is the Dark One's touch on the world |
| "You have no choice" | Moiraine's comfort | The chapter's title: whether anyone in it chooses is the question the series keeps asking |

## Craft lessons

1. **Compress a week into a montage, then stop for the scenes that matter.** The journey takes almost a week in a few paragraphs; the chapter slows only for the lessons, the quarrel, the threat, the eavesdropping and the gate.
2. **Let quiet create its own tension.** With no Trollocs in sight, the party raises its own conflicts: a braid, a word about Darkfriends, a dream of Illian.
3. **Give each teacher a lesson that shows who they are.** Lan teaches control, Thom teaches play, Moiraine teaches truth with limits. The pupils learn the reader's lessons at the same time.
4. **Hide a clue in a slip of the tongue.** "Two in one village" is said musingly and taken back at once. A reader who notices has Nynaeve's secret a long way early.
5. **Deliver the world through a gossip.** Avin's rambling carries five pieces of news, and his manner makes them feel overheard rather than explained.
6. **Plant prophecy as a puzzle the hero questions.** Rand finds the Stone of Tear riddle impossible; that is exactly what makes its later fulfilment satisfying.
7. **Title the chapter after its question.** "Choices" ends with Moiraine telling the boys they have none, after making every choice for them.
