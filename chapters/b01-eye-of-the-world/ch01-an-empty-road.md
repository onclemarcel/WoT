Chapter 1 of *The Eye of the World* plants the whole series in miniature: a winter that won't end, a rider whose cloak the wind doesn't touch, and two farm boys, Rand and Mat, who each saw him and were not believed. It follows the prologue "Dragonmount", and the drop from Lews Therin's death to a cart of cider on a muddy road is the series' scale in two scenes. The chapter is told entirely from Rand's point of view, the day before Bel Tine.

## Setting

The setting is calm on the surface and wrong underneath: spring should have come a month ago, and it hasn't. The chapter opens on the series' framing formula (the Wheel turns, Ages pass, legend fades to myth) and then follows a wind from the Mountains of Mist, across the Sand Hills (once an ocean shore, before the Breaking), down into the Westwood, where it reaches Rand and Tam.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Quarry Road, Westwood | Rand and Tam walk Bela and a cart of apple brandy and cider to the Winespring Inn; Rand has an arrow nocked, Tam uses his spear as a staff | No birds, no squirrels, pale sunlight "as if mixed with shadow": the forest Rand knew has become a place that can hide a watcher |
| The weather and the land | Wolves and bears driven down from the mountains, stillborn lambs, brown fields, ravens where songbirds used to be, no storks nesting | The Dark One's touch, not yet named; Cenn fears the winter will last until the Age ends |
| Emond's Field | Spring cleaning, children playing again, the Green half dead, the Spring Pole going up, bonfire stacks; Winternight tonight, Bel Tine tomorrow | A village that runs on custom ("the way it had always been") and stubbornness ("we'll survive, the Light willing") |
| The Winespring Inn | River-rock walls, the village's only red tile roof; Bran al'Vere's home and the Mayor's seat | Its foundation is older stone "some said came from the mountains", next to a much larger old foundation with a giant oak growing in it: the first trace of [WoT — Manetheren](../../world/manetheren.md) |

## Characters

Rand, Tam and Mat carry the chapter; the villagers are a comic chorus, and most of the main cast is named before they appear.

| Character | Mindset | Wants | Key moment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rand al'Thor | Uneasy, then shaken; doubts his own eyes but not the hatred he felt. Stubborn, dislikes being pushed | To get off the farm, enjoy Bel Tine, maybe place in the archery; to avoid the matchmaking goodwives and his own confusion about Egwene | The black rider stares at him with hatred "for him above all things", then vanishes; Rand realizes the wind never moved its cloak. Tam's reminder of the flame and the void doesn't hold |
| Tam al'Thor | A "pillar of reality": calm, practical, keeps his word | To deliver the brandy as promised, then a pipe and an ale | Doesn't call Rand a liar, offers to look for hoof prints, and quietly reminds him of the void, which shows he noticed the fear |
| Mat Cauthon | Mischievous and still a boy, but genuinely scared | Fun: a badger loose on the Green; then the gleeman and the fireworks | Saw the same rider three days earlier, outside the village. Jokes it might be the Dark One, names Ishamael and Aginor, then "maybe it was the Dragon" |
| Bran al'Vere | Mayor and innkeeper; shrewd and good-humoured | Raise the village's spirits | Brought the gleeman as a surprise; tells Rand he'll sit on the Village Council one day |
| Cenn Buie | Thatcher and Council member; a gnarled prophet of doom | To be listened to | Reads omens in the missing storks and Nynaeve's silence: maybe winter won't end until the Age ends |
| Wit and Daise Congar | Comic relief: a whining complainer and a wife who silences him | Wit wants the Council to deal with Nynaeve | Shows the Village Council vs. Women's Circle split |

**Named but not on stage:** Perrin Aybara (Rand's closest friend with Mat; the three once went to the foot of the Mountains of Mist), Egwene (the Mayor's daughter, who has made Rand jittery for a year; the chapter ends with him half dreading meeting her), Nynaeve al'Meara (a young Wisdom whose authority is disputed and who refuses to say what she hears on the wind), the gleeman (arrived alone, at night, which nobody does), and Rand's mother, an outlander whose grave he decorates. Moiraine and Lan first appear in Chapter 2.

## Craft lessons

1. **A framing device that moves the camera.** The wind starts at the Wheel of Time, crosses the map and lands on two men on a road: myth, geography and character in one paragraph.
2. **Calm first, then the intrusion.** Chores, gossip and festival preparations take their time, so the rider lands as a violation of that calm.
3. **One witness, then a second.** Rand alone sees the rider and doubts himself; Mat's matching account turns doubt into a shared secret neither can prove.
4. **Sense of wrongness through one physical detail.** The cloak that the wind doesn't move says "unnatural" without any explanation.
5. **Exposition through banter.** The Dark One, the Forsaken and the Dragon arrive as a catechism and a boy's nursery fears, not as a lecture.
6. **Comic chorus against dread.** The Congars, Cenn Buie and the matchmaking goodwives keep the village alive and make the threat stand out.
7. **Name the cast before showing it.** Perrin, Egwene and Nynaeve each get a line of reputation, so their entrances later feel like meetings, not introductions.
8. **End on a personal hook.** The chapter closes not on the rider but on Rand's nerves about Egwene.
