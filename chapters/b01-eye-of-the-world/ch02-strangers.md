Chapter 2 answers Chapter 1's single stranger with three more: a raven that won't be driven off, a lady and her swordsman, and a peddler at the end. Rand counts them as an "awful coincidence"; Mat only sees the best Bel Tine ever. Every newcomer is a sign the reader can't decode yet, and the chapter ends on cheer with a threat hidden inside it.

## Setting

The chapter moves from warm interiors to the open Green, and each space shows the village closing ranks against something it can't name.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The common room | A fire that "vanquished the chill", Bran's own ale, Scratch the cat, the books and stones boards idle | The whole Village Council huddled round Tam, talking low; Rand is kept out and never learns what about |
| The kitchen and cellar | Mistress al'Vere's bread and honeycakes; casks of Two Rivers ale and brandy, wine chalk-marked with the city it came from | A village proud of its own make, with the outside world present only as labels on barrels |
| The Green and the inn front | Festival bustle, the Spring Pole waiting, the inn "a thousand years" old | A raven on the roof that watches, dodges stones and flies west toward the Mountains of Mist |
| The Wagon Bridge | A crowd escorting the peddler's huge wagon, hung with bundles | The last arrival of the day, greeted as pure good news |

## Characters

Rand and Mat react to the same strangers in opposite ways: Rand counts and worries, Mat collects wonders. Moiraine and Lan enter, seen entirely from outside.

| Character | Mindset | Wants | Key moment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rand | Wary, still shaken by the rider; dazzled by Moiraine but asks the one awkward question | To know who the strangers are and why they came | Asks Moiraine why she is here; her smile fades "as if something had been recalled to her" |
| Mat | Restless, thrilled by novelty, dodging the consequences of his last prank | Fun: strangers, a gleeman, fireworks | "I don't care what they want"; yet he is the one who decides the coin must not be spent |
| Ewin Finngar | Fourteen, eager, gullible after Mat's ghost-hound story | To be believed and included | Delivers the news of the strangers, and their names, with his own storybook spin ("in service", "a Warder") |
| Moiraine | Gracious, ageless, in command; studies the boys while seeming to chat | Information: ages, families, how long people have lived here | Gives each youth a silver coin as a "token" of a bond; pauses to muse that men wear "many names, many faces" |
| Lan | Silent, watchful; walks "like a wolf" | To guard Moiraine | His cold blue gaze weighs the three youths and gives nothing away |
| Mistress al'Vere | Motherly, the best cook in the village | To feed every young man in reach | The one married woman who never matchmakes for Tam |
| The Village Council | Worried, closed; Haral Luhhan the blacksmith, Jon Thane the miller, Cenn, Rowan Hurn, Samel Crawe, with Bran and Tam | To decide something they won't share | Tam speaks, Bran presses a point, all nod; Cenn reluctantly |

**Named but not on stage:** Nynaeve, who met Moiraine that morning, was called "child" and did not take it well; Alsbet Luhhan and her broom; Adan al'Caar and Dag Coplin, the targets of Mat's prank; the gleeman, still asleep. Egwene appears only in a comparison (Moiraine's smile is the kind Egwene wears when she has a secret); Perrin is not mentioned, though his master Haral Luhhan sits on the Council.

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter is built on signs that mean nothing to Two Rivers eyes and everything to a returning reader.

| Sign | What the boys see | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moiraine's ageless face | A woman who looks young, then older the longer he looks | The mark of long work with the One Power: an Aes Sedai |
| The serpent ring | A gold ring of a serpent biting its tail | The Great Serpent ring every Aes Sedai wears |
| The silver coin | A fat coin showing a woman with a flame in her hand, worth a good horse | Money from Tar Valon, whose sign is the Flame. It is not payment: it is the bond she names, "there is a bond between us now", and she tells them to keep it on them |
| Her questions | Ages, families, how long people have lived where they live | She is hunting a young man of the right age, just as the Shadow is |
| "Many names, many faces" | Puzzling talk about history | The Dragon reborn: one soul across the turnings of the Wheel |
| Lan's cloak | A cloak that shifts colour and fades into the background | A Warder's cloak. Ewin's guess is right and Mat laughs it off. Warders serve Aes Sedai, so this is the chapter's clearest pointer to what Moiraine is, but only in hindsight: the words "Aes Sedai" never appear here |
| The raven | A bird that stares, sidesteps stones and flies west | A spy for the Shadow; Moiraine's arrival drives it off |
| The peddler | Fireworks and goods, the best news of the day | The peddler (named later as Padan Fain) is the Shadow's tool, and later his own |

Two cloaks frame the chapter's choice: Chapter 1's black cloak that the wind can't touch, and Lan's cloak that the eye can't hold. Both strangers are uncanny; only one is on Rand's side, and nothing yet tells him which.

## Moiraine's lines, read twice

Every line Moiraine speaks is true, and almost every one means more than the boys hear. Later the reader learns that Aes Sedai cannot speak a lie, which turns this scene into a lesson in truth used to mislead.

| She says | First reading | Second reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "A vile bird, to be mistrusted in the best of times" | A lady's dislike of carrion birds | She knows ravens spy for the Shadow, and her arrival drives this one off |
| "You must call me Moiraine, not lady" | Gracious modesty | She sets aside a rank she really holds, and hides the title that matters |
| "Some small tasks to be done ... Perhaps you would be willing to assist me?" | Errands for pocket money | The task is to leave the Two Rivers with her |
| "Keep it with you ... There is a bond between us now" | A quaint way to seal a deal | Exactly what the coin is: a bond, not a wage |
| "Later we must talk, and you must tell me all about yourselves" | Friendly curiosity | She is still sorting candidates: which of these young men is the one |
| "I am a student of history, a collector of old stories" | A scholar's hobby | True, and the history she means is Manetheren and the Dragon |
| "Places wear many names" | Small talk about "Two Rivers" | The Two Rivers was once Manetheren |
| "Men wear many names, many faces. Different faces, but always the same man" | Philosophy | The Dragon reborn: Lews Therin returned in a new face |
| "No one knows the Great Pattern ... We can only watch, and study, and hope" | A wistful aside | A summary of her long search, and of the whole series' theme |

One gesture also reads twice: she closes Rand's and Mat's hands around their coins with both of hers, but only hands Ewin his. The boy of fourteen is too young to be the one she is looking for.

## Craft lessons

1. **Mirror the menace.** Lan's shifting cloak answers the rider's unmoving one. Two uncanny strangers make the reader judge, and the hero can't yet tell them apart.
2. **Signs before names.** The serpent ring, the flame coin, the ageless face: the reader sees Aes Sedai long before the word is spoken, so the reveal feels earned.
3. **Let a minor character guess right.** Ewin's "Warder" is dismissed in the scene and confirmed later; the truth was on the page, in the least trusted mouth.
4. **Write dialogue for two readings.** Moiraine's lines are true and innocent on first reading, loaded on the second. A rule of the world (Aes Sedai can't lie) later explains why.
5. **Exposition through an unreliable messenger.** Ewin brings the news with his own storybook slant, which makes it lively and lets Mat argue with it.
6. **Two reactions to one event.** Rand's caution and Mat's appetite for novelty characterise both boys without a line of description.
7. **A gift that binds.** The coin is a hook the reader carries forward: what tasks, what bond, why both boys feel it must not be spent.
8. **Withhold, visibly.** The Council's huddle is shown but not heard. A closed door is a promise of later news.
9. **End on cheer with a threat inside.** "It was going to be the best Bel Tine ever", and the reader who suspects the peddler, the raven and the rider knows it won't be.
