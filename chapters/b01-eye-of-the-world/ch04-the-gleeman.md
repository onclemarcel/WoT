Chapter 4 brings the last stranger, the gleeman Thom Merrilin, and then sends Rand home. Thom's performance turns the outside world into a show of stories, but his wariness of Moiraine and his scorn for Fain say he knows more than he tells. The chapter ends on relief: Tam believes Rand about the black rider, and "that others knew and believed made all the difference". The reader, who knows a horror is coming on Winternight, feels that relief as a trap.

## Setting

The chapter moves from a crowd on the Green to a stable and then to an empty forest road, shrinking the world as the light fails. Along the way Tam's talk maps the Two Rivers' borders for the first time.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The old stone foundation | Thom's stage: he somersaults onto it and juggles | The ruin of Manetheren again, now under a teller of old tales |
| The Green before the inn | A score of villagers and children waiting for wonders | The crowd Moiraine joins unnoticed, with Lan seen only on a second look |
| The inn doorway | The Council and Nynaeve bursting out, quarrelling | Fain stays inside with his mulled wine, out of sight |
| The stable | Three strange horses, each matching its rider: a fierce black stallion, a dancing white mare, a rangy brown gelding | Horses that will carry their riders out of the Two Rivers within days |
| The Quarry Road | Leafless trees, wind, Rand with an arrow half drawn | The road the black rider has already used |

**The borders.** Only one road leads in, from the north by Taren Ferry. The Mountains of Mist close the west and the Mire closes the east. To the south, the White River churns over rocks, and beyond it the Forest of Shadows runs a hundred miles or more with no road or village. A land this closed explains why news arrives late and stories fill the gap.

## Characters

Thom Merrilin is the chapter's one new arrival, and every other character is measured by how they react to him. Tam, quiet until now, turns out to be the one adult who takes the rider seriously.

## Thom's stories, read twice

Thom's list of tales sounds like fantasy, but several are our own history worn smooth by time. They tell the attentive reader that the Wheel turns: our world is one of its Ages, long past or still to come.

| Thom's tale | First reading | Second reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lenn, who flew to the moon in the belly of an eagle made of fire | A fairy tale | John Glenn and the Apollo flights: the lunar module was named Eagle |
| Salya, walking among the stars | Lenn's daughter's adventure | Sally Ride, the first American woman in space |
| Mosk the Giant, his Lance of Fire that could reach around the world, and his wars with Alsbet, the Queen of All | A giant's war | Moscow's missiles and the Cold War against the West (Elizabeth) |
| Materese the Healer, Mother of the Wondrous Ind | A saint's legend | Mother Teresa of India |
| Ages ended by fire raining from the skies, and Ages doomed by snow and ice | Poetic doom | Nuclear war, and the ice ages |
| Ages when men ruled the heavens and the stars | Boasting | Spaceflight, our own Age seen from far away |
| Artur Paendrag Tanreall, Artur Hawkwing | A legendary High King | King Arthur Pendragon; Hawkwing's heirs will return as the Seanchan |
| The Dragon's "attempt to free the Dark One" and the Aes Sedai who "shattered the world" | Settled history | The village's half-truth again: the Dragon tried to seal the Dark One away, and it was the male Aes Sedai, driven mad, who broke the world |

The joke works on two levels. Rand thinks Egwene asks for Lenn and Salya to annoy him, since she usually prefers funny stories. In fact she is asking for tales about leaving home, which is exactly her plan.

## What the reader can't decode yet

Thom fills the chapter with jokes and boasts, and almost every one points at something true.

| Sign | What the village sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rand's grey eyes: "Everybody in the Two Rivers had dark eyes" | A boy teased for being odd | Rand was not born here; his eyes and height are Aiel |
| Thom: "as tall as an Aielman" | A gleeman's flattery | A plain statement of Rand's blood, made by a man who has seen Aiel |
| "There's more raven in him than man" (Thom on Fain) | A rival's scorn | Ravens spy for the Shadow, and so does Fain |
| "I make a point of never knowing anything about Aes Sedai. Much safer that way" | A showman dodging a question | Thom has history with Aes Sedai, and recognises one at once |
| Moiraine calls him "Master Bard" | Politeness | She knows exactly who he was: a court bard is a rank, not a compliment |
| "Once a Court bard" | Boasting | True: Thom served at the court of Andor, and fled it |
| Lan is seen only on a second look | Rand's inattention | The Warder's cloak at work again |
| Only young men see the rider | A mystery, or nerves | The Myrddraal shows itself to the young men of the right age: it is hunting one of them |
| Tam: "I've seen men die because they were sure" | A careful farmer | Tam was a soldier, and more than that, far from the Two Rivers |
| Tam leaves early for the farm | Prudence | The farm is where the danger will strike on Winternight |
| Rand's bow, which few men can draw | A strong young man | A weapon he made himself and can use; it will matter within a day |
| Cenn compared to "a black-veiled Aiel" | An insult from stories | Aiel veil their faces before they kill; the Aiel will matter to Rand more than to anyone |

## Reader's notes

Your close-reading notes on Chapter 4, with what the later books make of each. Tales are collected in [WoT — Tales and Legends](../../world/tales-and-legends.md).

| Note | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Thom's baggy sleeves and big pockets | A gleeman's costume on first reading; in fact they hide his juggling balls, his pipe, and the throwing knives he will use later. |
| Blue again: Lan's eyes, Moiraine's blue gown and stone, Thom's eyes | Blue marks outsiders in a land of dark eyes. Moiraine's blue also points to her Ajah, the Blue. That Lan and Thom, the two men who will be bound to her, both have blue eyes is a pleasing pattern, but the text never makes it a code. |
| Why is Thom in Emond's Field? | Officially the Council hired him for Festival. His irony ("exalted rank of Master Gleeman") shows a fallen court bard hiding in a backwater. Later he joins the flight partly to save a young man from Aes Sedai, as he could not save his nephew. The timing is also the Pattern at work: ta'veren draw people to them. |
| "As tall as an Aielman", then "almost the size of an Ogier" | The first is a true observation; the second is cover, to make the first sound like a joke. |
| Watch Hill, Deven Ride, Taren Ferry | All three are Two Rivers villages. Baerlon, Caemlyn and Lugard are the first places named beyond it. |
| Thom's history in one sentence | Collected, with every tale he names, in the Tales and Legends card. |
| Moiraine and Thom | Sharp on both sides. "Some stories I like, and some I do not" is a warning from a woman who cannot lie. "A simple gleeman; that and nothing more" is a lie from a man who can. |
| Nynaeve is never soft | Her anger is her authority, and often her fear. The reader sees her tenderness only later, in her care for the boys she follows out of the Two Rivers and in her love for Lan. |
| Tam: "Battles don't interest me" | True of a man who has fought them. |
| "We can see no reason for Aes Sedai to come here" | The text never says Tam recognised Moiraine. He had seen Aes Sedai in the Aiel War, and he found Rand on Dragonmount; strangers asking about young men would worry him. Read it as Council reasoning that Tam may privately doubt. |
| Tam takes Rand home for Winternight | His stated reason: with both away, the stranger could make mischief at the farm. It also takes Rand away from the strangers. It will put both of them where the attack falls. |
| "Tinkers" | Mat names them without explanation: the Tuatha'an, wandering people of the Way of the Leaf, met in Book 1. |
| Tam believes the black rider | The trigger is in the text: four solid lads, Rand, Perrin, Lem Thane and Ban Crawe, all saw it, and "only young men see this fellow". He reasons like a soldier from reports, not from foreknowledge. |

## Craft lessons

1. **Introduce a character through a performance.** Thom's rant, juggling and somersault tell us he is vain, skilled and hiding something, without a line of description of his past.
2. **Hide the real world in the legends.** Lenn, Salya, Mosk and Materese reward readers who notice, and state the series' cyclical time without a lecture.
3. **Let one character's reaction reveal another.** Thom's stopped juggling tells us more about Moiraine than anything she says.
4. **Plant the hero's secret in a joke.** "As tall as an Aielman" is played for laughs and is literally true.
5. **Use a quarrel to show an institution.** Nynaeve against Bran, and Tam's explanation in the cart, show how the village governs itself.
6. **End on relief the reader distrusts.** "That others knew and believed made all the difference" calms Rand, while the reader knows the rider is still out there.
7. **Give a father hidden depth.** Tam's lines about seeing men die make him more than a farmer, before the story says why.
