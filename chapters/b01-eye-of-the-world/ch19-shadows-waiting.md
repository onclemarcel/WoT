Chapter 19 shows that the refuge from the Shadow holds an evil of its own. While Nynaeve's herbs restore Moiraine, Mat, Rand and Perrin slip away to explore the ruined city. A bald stranger named Mordeth offers them treasure, flies into rage at the words "Tar Valon", and is unmasked because he casts no shadow. He swells to fill the room, then vanishes like smoke into a crack: "You are all dead!" Unseen watchers follow the boys back through the dusk. Moiraine tells how Aridhol destroyed itself and became Shadar Logoth, and asks whether Mordeth gave them anything. They say no, though Mat came out of the treasure room holding a dagger. Then Lan returns: over a hundred Trollocs are inside the walls, and the party must cross the city at night to reach the river.

## Setting

Shadar Logoth is the grandest place the boys have ever seen and the emptiest, and the chapter makes the two the same thing.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The streets of the dead city | Broken paving; marble palaces with domes of every shape; colonnades, bronze fountains, statues; not one pigeon | Eyes in every doorway once the sun goes down |
| The white stone shell | A huge room with dirty tiles and rotten hangings; a back room with dust no animal has crossed | Moiraine's wards, which the boys leave without knowing |
| The round dome and the stone benches | A hall "as big as a hill"; an open-air place with rows of seats | The people Rand imagines in the shadows, disapproving |
| The colonnaded steps | A place to sit and argue in the last sun | Mordeth, in the shadows behind the columns |
| The stair down and the torchlit corridor | Pitch dark, then smoky torches in iron sconces | Many shadows for each boy, none for Mordeth |
| The treasure room | Waist-high heaps of gold, gems, goblets, jewelled swords and daggers | A trap: gifts that "twist the mind and taint the spirit" |
| The streets at dusk | Long, jagged shadows like reaching hands | Watchers, "eager, hungry", and a dry rustling laughter |
| The ward line | Nothing visible | A boundary the city's evil cannot cross, and Myrddraal cannot even sense |

## How Aridhol became Shadar Logoth

Moiraine's tale makes the city's evil a moral one: "No enemy had come to Aridhol but Aridhol." Suspicion and hate, used in the Light's name, bred something worse than the Shadow.

1. **An ally.** Aridhol is one of the Ten Nations of the Second Covenant. Its king, Balwen Ironhand, reigns when Thorin al Toren al Ban rules Manetheren.
2. **A counsellor.** In a dark hour of the Trolloc Wars, Mordeth comes to Balwen's court and rises to second only to the king.
3. **A war cry.** "The victory of the Light is all." Aridhol shouts it while its deeds abandon the Light; some would rather meet Trollocs than its men.
4. **An embassy betrayed.** Thorin's son Caar comes to win Aridhol back. Mad Balwen laughs while Mordeth orders Caar's death as a Friend of the Dark. Caar escapes, loses a hand, and flees to the Borderlands.
5. **A tale within the tale.** There Caar marries Rhea, who does not know him; she kills him, then herself at his tomb, and Aleth-Loriel falls (the title Thom told in Ch. 17).
6. **The end.** Manetheren's armies find the gates torn down and no living thing inside. Mashadar, born of the city's own hate, waits in the bedrock. Men call it Shadar Logoth, "the Place Where the Shadow Waits".
7. **A Shadow army lost.** Late in the war, Trollocs, Darkfriends, Myrddraal and Dreadlords camp in the ruins. Scouts find only blood, weapons and pleas to the Dark One scratched on walls, later scoured away. Shadowspawn remember.

**Mordeth's rules.** Moiraine's questions to the boys are a list of what the city can do to them.

| Rule | What it means for the party |
| --- | --- |
| His gifts "twist the mind and taint the spirit", waxing and waning until they rule or kill | Hence "Did he give you anything?" |
| Touch, or doing something for him, gives him a hold | Hence "Did he touch you?" and the treasure he wanted carried |
| If he leads someone to the walls, he consumes that soul and leaves in that body | His horses were "outside the city somewhere" |
| Neither he nor anything else of the city crosses Moiraine's wards | The building is safe; the streets are not |
| They cannot stand the sun | The city is safe by day; at dusk the watchers follow the boys, and vanish only at the ward line |
| The wards are not built for Myrddraal, which cannot sense them | A Power-made defense against the Trollocs would wake what the wards hold back |

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter's quietest clue is the loudest for the series: Mat leaves the treasure room "clutching a dagger snatched from the trove", and when Moiraine asks, all three say Mordeth gave them nothing.

| Sign | What the party sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mat's dagger from the treasure | Nothing; no one mentions it | A gift of Shadar Logoth taken all the same; its taint will grow in Mat |
| "And at least I -" Mat starts, cut off by Perrin | Bravado | Often read as Mat about to admit he kept something |
| Mat's glum, faraway stare after the war cry | Tiredness | The old blood unsettles him; it makes him reckless here |
| Mordeth's rage at "Tar Valon" | A madman | He fears Aes Sedai, who could unmake his chance to escape |
| "He only wanted his hand" in Rand's dream; an old man cackling | A nightmare | Echoes Caar One-Hand; often read as a hint of Rand's own later loss, and of Ba'alzamon watching |
| A cradle on Rand's back and Tam's sword in a child's hands | A nightmare | Mixes Lan's cradle sword with Rand's own unknown birth |
| "A sword placed in his infant hands" | Moiraine reassuring Nynaeve | Confirms Min's viewing of Lan (Ch. 15): Malkier pledged him to the fight from birth |
| "I would know the minute of his death and the way of it" | Aes Sedai lore | The bond between Aes Sedai and Warder |
| "What forced the Myrddraal?" | Lan's question | Something stronger than a Fade drives them; Egwene guesses the Dark One, Moiraine says no |
| Thom's fear at "Aemon come back again" | An old man's caution | Rebirth is real in this world, as the Dragon's is; the text does not yet say how |
| Mordeth's treasure, "more than I can carry" | Greed | Bait; the boys come close to being carried out in his place |
| Mashadar "locked in the bedrock" | A legend | A real thing in the city, still to be seen |

## Craft lessons

1. **Make the refuge the trap.** The one place Trollocs fear is safe from Trollocs only. Readers learn the rule of this world: no place is simply safe.
2. **Let the young disobey.** The boys slip out while the adults are busy. Their escapade sets up the danger and, quietly, the dagger.
3. **Reveal the monster through a missing detail.** Rand counts shadows and finds none for Mordeth. A plain physical fact does more than any description of menace.
4. **Hide the plant in an action line.** Mat clutching a dagger is one clause in a fight. When Moiraine asks about gifts, the reader may not notice the lie.
5. **Tell history as a moral.** Aridhol fell not to Trollocs but to itself. The tale gives the series its warning that fighting the Shadow can turn a people into it.
6. **Tie an old title to a new tale.** "The Fall of Aleth-Loriel", a name in Thom's list two chapters ago, gets its story here. The world feels lived in because its stories connect.
7. **End with a forced move.** Trollocs inside the walls, the wards useless against them: the party must cross the dead city at night.
