Chapter 10 assembles the party and takes it out of the village. In a dim stable, three boys with three weapons wait for a Warder; then Egwene walks in uninvited and Thom drops down from the hayloft, and Moiraine accepts both as "part of the Pattern". They ford the Winespring Water, dodge the village's own patrols, and look back at home. Then a Draghkar crosses the moon, and the leavetaking becomes a flight up the North Road.

## Setting

The chapter is a slow exit through home, one boundary at a time: the stable door, the stream, the last houses, the road. Each place is familiar, and each is seen for the last time.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Winespring Inn stable | One half-shuttered lantern, deep shadows, five saddled horses, a hayloft | Two uninvited travellers: Egwene at the door, Thom in the loft Perrin forgot to check |
| The stableyard and the inn's shadow | Moon shadows; the back of the inn dark and silent | The last moment the party could be stopped |
| The Wagon Bridge and the inn's front | A dozen villagers in attic armour, marching "to three different drums" | The village now guards itself, too weakly to stop Trollocs, well enough to stop the boys |
| The Winespring Water | Cold, swift, deep enough to lap the stirrups near its source | The crossing that leaves the village behind |
| Emond's Field by night | Lit windows with watchers inside; high-peaked houses Rand tries to memorize | Home already turning into memory: "already he was homesick" |
| The countryside and the North Road | A Two Rivers sky "like points of light scattered through crystal"; the road to Taren Ferry | A Draghkar hunting across the moon; the road is no longer safe |

## Characters

Seven travellers leave together for the first time: three hunted boys, a girl who chose to come, an Aes Sedai, her Warder and a gleeman. Each arrives with one object that says who they are.

| Character | Mindset | Wants | Key moment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rand | Shaky, homesick, trying to look eager | To keep Egwene safe, and to keep looking at home | Tells Moiraine he told Tam; sees the Draghkar cross the moon |
| Mat | Joking to cover fear | To believe this is an adventure | "It isn't much like the stories"; his mother thinks Tar Valon is "the next thing to Shayol Ghul" |
| Perrin | Defensive, careful, sour | To keep his axe and his practising private | Shows the half-moon axe Master Luhhan gave him |
| Egwene | Defiant, clear-headed, warm | To see the world: "I've dreamed about it as long as you have" | Works out the whole plan from a horse purchase; "We will look after each other" |
| Moiraine | Sharp, quick, unsurprised | Speed, secrecy, and whatever the Pattern brings | "It is part of the Pattern, now, Lan": she lets Egwene come |
| Lan | Cold, watchful, overruled | The party alive, and no extra mouths | "Anything can be a weapon"; draws his sword twice; names the Draghkar |
| Thom | Theatrical, wary of Lan's blade | To get out of a village with no use for him, and to perform in Tar Valon | Climbs down from the loft: "I am not a cheese for slicing" |

**Named but not on stage:** Tam, who agreed Rand had to go; Master and Mistress Luhhan, "stubborn as stone", she hoping the Trollocs come back; Mat's mother, who would lock him in the cellar; Jon Thane, leading a patrol in a jerkin sewn with steel disks, and owner of Cloud; Bela, saddled for the first time; the Mayor, whom Rand asked about the farms; the unnamed wool-buyer's guard who would not pay for Perrin's axe.

## The party assembles

The chapter builds the fellowship one arrival at a time, and every arrival tests Moiraine's control. The boys were invited; Egwene and Thom invite themselves; Moiraine absorbs both with the same answer.

| Who | How they join | What they carry | Moiraine's answer | Lan's reaction |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Perrin | Already waiting, told to search the stable | A half-moon axe Master Luhhan made for a guard who would not pay | (none needed) | Missed the loft |
| Mat | Arrives with Lan, cloaked, rocking on his heels | His bow and a quiver | (none needed) | "That bow is a real weapon" |
| Rand | Last, from his father's room | Tam's heron-mark sword | "What's done is already woven in the Pattern": he told Tam | Silent |
| Egwene | Walks in with a bundle, having deduced everything | Food, a shawl-wrapped bundle, shoes unfit for the road | "You may come with us"; "It is part of the Pattern, now" | "No, Moiraine!": the only time he shouts |
| Thom | Drops a blanket-roll from the hayloft | Flute, harp and saddlebags | "Everything is a part of the Pattern... We cannot pick and choose. But we shall see" | Sword out, and kept out |

**What the weapons say.** The sword, the axe and the bow are Rand's, Perrin's and Mat's first signatures, and the chapter teases each. Lan ends the joking: "Anything can be a weapon, if the man or woman who holds it has the nerve and will to make it so."

**Who is missing.** Nynaeve does not appear and is not mentioned. The party that rides out is seven, and the reader has no reason yet to count an eighth.

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter's code is in its small reactions: a look from Moiraine, a shout from Lan, a word from Thom, a single exclamation from Perrin.

| Sign | What the party sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moiraine studies Egwene, tapping her lips, then says "There is a reason for it" | An Aes Sedai's whim | Moiraine has sensed that Egwene can channel; she will train for the Tower |
| Lan's "No, Moiraine!", his only outburst | A Warder's caution | He knows the risk of an extra traveller, and that Moiraine is choosing for reasons of her own |
| "What's done is already woven in the Pattern" when Rand admits he told Tam | Acceptance | Moiraine wanted no one to know; she makes the best of a choice she did not want |
| Thom: "worse than Trollocs and Halfmen were created", and Moiraine's sharp look | A gleeman's lore | Thom knows more than a gleeman should; his past is more than villages and inns |
| Perrin's "Wolves!", and Lan: wolves and Trollocs hate each other | Fear of wolves | Wolves will become Perrin's allies, and his burden |
| Perrin "practicing" with the axe, and hiding it | Embarrassment | The axe is the centre of Perrin's conflict about violence |
| "He and his Trollocs will not cross as easily as we" at Taren Ferry | Common sense | Trollocs fear deep water |
| The staff shows no charring after the flames | Odd | The fire was the One Power, not burning wood |
| A shape "as large as a man" hunting across the moon | A bat | A Draghkar, a Shadowspawn made in the war that ended the Age of Legends; it finds prey for the Myrddraal |
| "Two Dha'vol Trollocs would have them all for breakfast" | Lan's contempt | Another Trolloc band, after Lan's Ko'bal of Chapter 7 |
| Nynaeve is never mentioned | Nothing | The Wisdom is not done with them; the party is not yet complete |

## Craft lessons

1. **Assemble the party on stage.** Each traveller arrives in a different way, so the reader meets the fellowship one person at a time and learns its tensions before the road begins.
2. **Give each hero an object.** Sword, axe, bow: one prop per boy fixes who they are faster than any description.
3. **Let the uninvited force the plan.** Egwene and Thom are not in Moiraine's design. Their arrival shows the plan is already bending, and that Moiraine bends with it.
4. **Use a logical deduction to show a character.** Egwene works it all out from a horse purchase and two sneaking boys. One paragraph makes her an equal.
5. **Make home visible as it is lost.** Rand's homesickness before he has left the village turns setting into feeling.
6. **Turn a departure into a chase.** The Draghkar changes the pace from creeping to galloping in one image.
7. **Leave someone out on purpose.** Nynaeve's absence is invisible now and obvious later, which makes her return a surprise the text has fairly prepared.
