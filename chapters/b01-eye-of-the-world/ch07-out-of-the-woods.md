Chapter 7 takes away every safe place Rand was walking toward, one at a time, until only the most feared one is left. Emond's Field is half burned, the Wisdom says she can do nothing, and the Mayor has no answer. The only help is an Aes Sedai, the villain of every story, who saved the village the night before. The chapter ends with Rand committed to her, promising "any price" he can pay.

## Setting

The village Rand pictured all night, lit for Winternight, is now half ash. The chapter walks him through it in a line: the edge of the woods, the Green, the Calder house, the inn, and out to the bonfires, each stop taking away one more hope.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emond's Field at dawn | Half the houses burned, chimneys like "dirty fingers", rescued furniture in the street; five houses whole in one row, one alone in another | Destruction "scattered at random", or not: the attack chose its targets |
| Abell Cauthon's and Berin Thane's houses | Only foundations and ash on either side of the Calder house | Mat's home is gone; the reader learns he is alive only by a word |
| The Calder house | Mistress Calder's makeshift infirmary; Nynaeve and Egwene with clean hands and dirty faces | Where the Wisdom says "There's nothing I can do" |
| The Winespring Inn | Barely scorched, red tiles bright; the Dragon's Fang chalked on its door; the peddler's wagon burned to its rims | The inn the Trollocs did not burn shelters the Aes Sedai, and someone has marked it |
| The old foundation | Thom on the stones, trimming singed patches from his cloak | The Manetheren ruin again, now a seat beside a battle's wreckage |
| The upstairs room | A soft bed, a fire, the window on the Green | The place Tam will lie while Rand bargains for his life |
| The Bel Tine bonfires, north of the village | Three festival fires now burning dead Trollocs; men in vinegar masks; a sickly sweet stink | Festival turned into a funeral pyre; Lan reading Trolloc badges |

## The stories against the woman

Chapter 5 tested the tales of Trollocs against what Rand saw; Chapter 7 does the same for Aes Sedai, and the result runs the other way. The Trolloc stories were too mild. The Aes Sedai stories are too dark, but not wholly wrong, and the chapter never lets Rand, or the reader, settle which.

| What the stories say | What the chapter shows |
| --- | --- |
| Aes Sedai broke the world; there is hardly a story where one is not a villain | Moiraine called ball lightning down on the Trollocs; without her and Lan "there would be any village at all left" |
| If the outside showed the inside, she would look "closer to a Trolloc" | A tired, handsome woman with soot on her nose, sitting in the dirt, her dignity untouched |
| "Did you think they wore signs?" (Bran) | She was in the village for days and no one knew |
| Their help is "like poison in a pie"; their gifts have a hook "like fishbait" | "We will speak of prices later, Rand, if at all"; Rand's coin suddenly burns in his pocket |
| Puppeteers who make thrones and nations dance | Her first question is not about Tam but about Rand: "How are your dreams?" |
| They can heal where medicines fail | She agrees to try, with no promises: she cannot "stop the Wheel from turning" |
| Nobody wants to be involved with them | "Too many here have refused to let me help at all" |

**Thom's warning** sums it up: "Aes Sedai do what they do for reasons of their own, and they aren't always the reasons others think." The stories are wrong about her heart and right about her reasons.

## What the reader can't decode yet

The village reads the night as a random disaster. Nearly every detail says it was a hunt with three targets.

| Sign | What the village sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Destruction "scattered at random"; Mat's house burned to the stones; the Trollocs went after the forge "as if I had gold and jewels in there" | Bad luck | The Trollocs struck the homes of the three boys: the al'Thor farm, the Cauthon house, and the forge where Perrin works |
| "Seven bands"; the Ko'bal trident badge; "not since the Trolloc Wars" | Lan's soldiering | A force not seen since Manetheren fell, sent for one target |
| "I thought we had gained a march, but we may be further behind than ever" | Tired talk | Moiraine is racing the Shadow to the same young man |
| "A little more light in the darkness" | A murmur | Another of the boys survived: her search is still alive |
| "How are your dreams, Rand al'Thor?" | An odd question | The Dark One reaches the boys through their dreams; she is checking whether he already has |
| "I don't know that you are worth it, sheepherder, no matter what she says" | Lan's temper | Moiraine has told Lan that Rand matters |
| "Even with an angreal" | A word no one explains | An object that lets an Aes Sedai channel more of the Power than she could alone |
| The Dragon's Fang chalked on the inn door | A Coplin or Congar insult | An accusation of evil aimed at the Mayor's guests, the Aes Sedai |
| "Old Fain just disappeared"; "Trollocs will eat anything" | A sad death | Fain left with the Shadow he had led there |
| Nynaeve can do nothing for a small cut | The fever is too far gone | A Shadowspawn blade's wound; Nynaeve has more in her than herbs, but not enough yet |
| Haral knows they are Trollocs at once | Common sense | The stories have just become knowledge for everyone, not only for Tam |

## Craft lessons

1. **Let the haven fall.** All of Chapter 6 points at the village as safety. Burning it removes the last fixed point and forces the hero toward the unknown.
2. **Strip away the helpers one by one.** Smith, Wisdom, Mayor: each fails a little more, so the last option, the one Rand fears, is the only one left.
3. **Tell the battle afterward.** The night's fight arrives in Bran's account and Lan's badge count. The reader gets its shape without the chapter losing Rand's point of view.
4. **Show shock through odd details.** Egwene's crooked buttons and clean hands say more about Rand's state than any statement of numbness.
5. **Reveal the ally the reader already knew.** The reader guessed Moiraine was Aes Sedai in Chapter 2; the village learns it now. The payoff is in watching Rand reconcile the stories with the woman.
6. **Let a character ask the wrong question.** "How are your dreams?" ignores Rand's plea and tells the reader her priorities.
7. **End on a bargain.** "Any price" is a hook the reader will carry until the price is named.
