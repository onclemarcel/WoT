Chapter 17 lets the party laugh for one evening and then takes it all back in one night. Thom tells the Great Hunt of the Horn and plays for the dancing; Rand dances with Nynaeve, Moiraine and a silent Egwene, while a scar-faced man watches him. Alone in the hall with a mug of milk, Rand meets a Fade. It lowers its black blade, says he belongs to the Great Lord of the Dark, and vanishes into the shadows as Lan comes down the stairs. The party flees at once. At the Caemlyn Gate, Bornhald tries to arrest them, and Moiraine grows taller than the town wall. An hour down the road the Stag and Lion is burning, and Nynaeve and Lan argue over the price.

## Setting

The chapter moves from the most crowded room in Baerlon to an empty hall, then out through a shut gate into the dark, and each step outward is more dangerous.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Stag and Lion common room | Every seat taken; Thom's tales, then flute, drum and dulcimer; a reel, a jig, laughing dancers | A scar-faced man by the fire who never smiles and keeps staring |
| The hall by the kitchen | A scullery maid, a stone crock, a mug of milk | A Fade, and shadows at the end of the hall that "reached out and embraced it" |
| The stableyard at night | Lamps, grumbling ostlers, Fitch bowing | Mutch, lamp raised, watching them go |
| The streets of Baerlon | Shuttered windows, barking dogs, a moon in its last quarter | Moon shadows that look like the hall's |
| The Caemlyn Gate | A guardhouse, three sleepy Watchmen, a crank-and-ratchet | Five Whitecloaks waiting in the dark; Moiraine taller than the wall |
| The Caemlyn Road, east | Hard-packed earth; Baerlon's glow on the clouds | The burning inn, and whoever set it |
| The cold camp | Roots and stones; one hour of sleep | Lan somewhere in the dark; no dreams, or none remembered |

## Two hunters in one night

The title has two kinds of hunter, and the chapter sets them side by side: the Shadow's servant in the hall and the Light's servant at the gate. Each corners Rand; each is stopped by a power greater than his own.

|  | The Fade, in the hall | Bornhald, at the gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How it comes | Out of nowhere, unhurried; its cloak hangs still as it moves | Out of the dark, five cowled men, hands on swords |
| What it wants | "Where are the others?" | Darkfriends to question at the camp |
| What it says Rand is | "You belong to the Great Lord of the Dark" | A Darkfriend escaping the Light |
| What Rand does | Freezes; cannot shout; forgets his sword | Hunches lower, which draws the eye |
| What stops it | Lan's boots on the stairs; it stops its own blade | Moiraine's staff and her looming shadow |
| How it ends | The shadows take it in | Five men in a heap, a sword melted almost in two |

The parallel is the point: to Rand, both hunters claim him for the Shadow. Only one is right about who wants him.

**The price, argued on the road.** The burning inn turns the chapter's last scene into a debate on what the mission costs other people.

| Voice | Position |
| --- | --- |
| Egwene | "Poor Master Fitch's inn is burning!" |
| Nynaeve | The innkeeper's loss is Moiraine's fault; walking in the Light means sparing a thought for him |
| Lan | It is the boys' doing, not hers; "the fact that the price must be paid is proof that it is worth paying" |
| Moiraine | Lays out the choices: go back alone and leave them with only Lan, or all go back into the Whitecloaks and the fire-setters. Then sends untraceable gold to rebuild the inn |
| Perrin | "She should have done something"; no one answers |

Moiraine wins the argument and loses the boys a little. The chapter does not tell the reader who is right.

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter's biggest mystery is a blade that stops: a Fade has Rand helpless and does not kill him.

| Sign | What the party sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Fade's blade stops at Rand's head: "You are his" | Lan's boots scared it off | The Shadow wants Rand alive, or at least not killed by a Fade; the text never says why |
| "You are one of them, boy" | It knows who they are | It knows there are several, but not which is the one, as in the shared dream |
| The shadows "reached out and embraced it"; Lan: "It's going; fading" | It ran | Fades can use shadows to travel; the text does not yet explain it |
| Rand cannot move or scream under its eyeless gaze | Fear | The Myrddraal's gaze itself paralyses with terror |
| Moiraine: "how we were found" | A mystery | Fain asked where they stayed and how long (Ch. 15); often read as his report |
| The scar-faced man | A Whitecloak spy, says Fitch | Lan is still bothered; the text never says whether he is more than that |
| Mutch watching with his lamp | A sour stableman | Rand is sure he will tell everything; whether he does, the text does not say |
| Moiraine taller than the wall; "The mind plays tricks in the night" | Something only Egwene dares to name | She answers with a saying, not a denial; often read as an illusion made with the Power |
| Bornhald's sword melted, the staff without a nick | Aes Sedai magic | The One Power against plain steel |
| "The Dark One's handiwork seen flying over the town" | Whitecloak talk | The Draghkar is still tracking them |
| "If only the Myrddraal were truly blind" | A joke | Fades see without eyes |
| "Remember what – who – it is that he wants" | A slip of the tongue | Moiraine is thinking of one person, not three |
| Darkfriends "in the highest councils, too" | A warning | The Shadow's servants are inside the halls of power |
| The Stag and Lion burns | Coincidence? | The Shadow, or its Darkfriends, came for them and found them gone |

## Craft lessons

1. **Give the reader a party before the attack.** Tales, a reel, a wink from a girl. The joy makes the empty hall afterwards feel colder.
2. **Plant a red herring in the crowd.** The scar-faced man draws the eye all evening, so the real danger arrives from where no one was looking.
3. **Take away the hero's weapon by fear, not force.** Rand wears Tam's sword the whole time and forgets it. The paralysis tells the reader what a Fade is better than any description.
4. **Let the villain spare the hero and say why, half.** "You are his" raises a bigger question than a near death would.
5. **Mirror two antagonists.** The Fade and Bornhald both corner Rand and both claim him for the Shadow; the reader learns the Light's servants can be as blind as they are fierce.
6. **Show a mentor's full power once, then deny it.** Moiraine grows taller than the wall, then says "the mind plays tricks". The awe stays, and so does the distrust.
7. **Make the cost visible on the horizon.** The fire glows behind them for an hour. The argument about it gives each character a moral position.
