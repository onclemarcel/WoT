Chapter 15 proves the dream was real and then sends Rand into the town half out of his mind. The inn's cook complains of a dozen dead rats, all with broken backs, and Perrin and Mat turn out to have had the same dream. In the stableyard Min reads Rand and his friends in images. In the streets Rand finds Padan Fain, alive and in rags, and promises not to tell Moiraine. Then a strange fever lets him laugh in the face of three Whitecloaks and their leader, Bornhald. Thom hears the dream and advises silence. The chapter ends at the inn door: Nynaeve has followed them to Baerlon.

## Setting

The town Rand dreamed of for years turns out to be crowds of strangers, and every place in it holds someone who knows more about him than he does.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rand's room at noon | Empty beds, warm water, the sun high; aches and a headache back | The other dreams he cannot remember |
| The Stag and Lion kitchen | Sara the cook defending her cat Cirri against Master Fitch; bread and cheese | A dozen dead rats "all with backs broken" |
| The common room | Painted walls, a hearth on every side; Thom on a table chanting the Great Hunt of the Horn | Nothing; Rand shuts the door without going in |
| The boys' room | Perrin in bed, sleepless; Mat's bow in the corner | The same dream, kept secret |
| The stableyard | An upended barrel, Mutch scowling | Min, who knows Moiraine's real name |
| The streets of Baerlon | Packed crowds of strangers; flagstones and mud; shops; nine inns; turnips priced like gold; faces like Two Rivers neighbours | Padan Fain in rags, and three Whitecloaks clearing the crowd around them |
| A dead-end alley | A tall fence | Fain's fear of "the Aes Sedai", and his bargaining |
| A second alley | Crates and a starved yellow dog | Thom's warning that some names are dangerous to say |

## Min's viewings

Min's readings are a list of the series' future packed into one conversation, and Rand treats it as foolishness. She says Moiraine calls it seeing "pieces of the Pattern"; she herself only sees images and sometimes knows what they mean. The middle column is the text; the last is from memory and marks what a first reader cannot know.

| Who | What Min sees | What it may point to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All of them together | Thousands of sparks swirling around a shadow "darker than midnight"; each trying to fill or swallow the other | A shared fate against the Shadow: Egwene and Thom are "part of it" too |
| Egwene | The same as Moiraine; "She won't refuse it"; she and Rand love each other but are not for each other | She will become Aes Sedai, and their love will not end in marriage |
| Lan | Seven ruined towers; a babe in a cradle holding a sword; too many images to read | Malkier's seven towers, lost when he was a baby, and the sword given to him in his cradle |
| Thom | A man, not him, juggling fire; the White Tower, "for a man" | The text never says; often read as a channeler Thom will be close to, and his tie to the Tower through Moiraine |
| Perrin | A wolf; a broken crown; trees flowering around him | The wolves he will meet on the road, and a lordship he will not want |
| Mat | A red eagle; an eye on a balance scale; a dagger with a ruby; a horn; a laughing face | Manetheren's banner; a price paid in an eye; a cursed dagger; the Horn of Valere; his luck |
| Rand | A sword that isn't a sword; a golden laurel crown; a beggar's staff; water poured on sand; a bloody hand and a white-hot iron; three women over his funeral bier; black rock wet with blood; lightning striking at him and coming out of him | Callandor; a crown; the Aiel Waste; a lost hand and a brand; the three women of Min's own life; Shayol Ghul; the Power |
| Min herself, with Rand | "You and I will meet again" | She is one of the three women |

**What Rand hears.** Love talk and nonsense, until "You can't escape from me", which is too close to Ba'alzamon's "You cannot get away from me". He runs.

## The dream made real, and Rand's fever

The dead rats turn Chapter 14's nightmare into a fact, and from that moment Rand's body starts behaving like someone else's. The chapter tracks it in stages.

| Stage | What happens | What Rand feels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Waking | Headache, aches back, "more dreams" he cannot recall | Annoyed to have slept late |
| The kitchen | Over a dozen dead rats, backs broken, more than a cat could leave | The bread "turned to ashes" in his mouth |
| Perrin, then Mat | "Did he kill a rat?" "You, too?" All three had the dream, with small differences | Guilty relief that he is not alone |
| Min and Fain | A seer, then a ghost from home | His head "packed with wool"; then like a balloon |
| Drifting | Mat tugs him along; he talks of Min | Not tired, just "drifting"; no fear of the One Power being used on him |
| Mat's prank | A sling stone breaks a cart stake; barrels splash three Whitecloaks | He looks forward to it, though "something told him that feeling was wrong" |
| Bornhald | He sweeps back his cloak, shows the heron-mark sword, asks for "a good inn" | A tingling thrill, heat like a fever, "nearly bursting" with energy |
| The Watch arrives | The Whitecloaks leave: "We will meet again" | Then it vanishes "like a pricked bubble", and he staggers |

**Fain in the alley.** The peddler is alive, gaunt, in rags, and refuses to come near Rand. His story: Trollocs burned his wagon, he could not reach his horses, he has barely slept or eaten since. He spits at the thought of "the Aes Sedai", makes Rand promise not to tell her, and asks how long the party stays at the Stag and Lion. "Tomorrow" seems to decide something. Mat later shrugs it off; the reader should not.

**Thom's counsel.** Thom hears the dream in a back alley and treats it as dangerous, not mad.

- **The names.** Davian, Yurian Stonebow, Guaire Amalasan, Raolin Darksbane: "Men who shook the pillars of heaven". All dead but Logain; Raolin Darksbane nearly two thousand years ago. "You might say the White Tower killed them", but used by the Amyrlin? He cannot see it.
- **The Eye of the World.** "A legend. Maybe." Young men in the Borderlands hunt it as young men in Illian hunt the Horn of Valere.
- **His advice.** Hold your peace for now: once you tell, you are "tied up worse than ever" with her. Then he rushes them back to warn Perrin.

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter's loudest clue is Rand's fever, and the text describes it in the same words it used for Bela's run in Chapter 11.

| Sign | What the party sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tingling, heat, "nearly bursting" with energy, then gone "like a pricked bubble" | A cold, or madness | Rand touching saidin unknowing, as with Bela (Ch. 11); the drifting and recklessness are early signs of what the Power does to a man |
| A dozen rats with broken backs | A bad cat, or a strange one | Ba'alzamon's reach goes beyond dreams into the waking world |
| All three boys had the dream, with small differences | Coincidence, or a curse | Ba'alzamon is searching all three because he does not know which is "the one" |
| Fain asks where they stay and how long | A frightened man's question | Fain is the Shadow's hound, sent to find them; he will report this |
| "Padan Fain knows which way to jump" | A boast | He survived Winternight by going with the Trollocs, not by escaping them |
| Fain will not come close to Rand | Fear | Often read as fear of what he serves, or of being sensed; the text never says |
| "Heron-mark, Lord Bornhald"; "He is too young" | Whitecloak bluster | The heron mark is a blademaster's sign; Bornhald's grudge will follow Rand and Perrin |
| "Gray-eyed bumpkin" | An insult | Rand's grey eyes, again marking him as not Two Rivers blood |
| Rand's look-alikes of Two Rivers folk in the crowd | Homesickness | The Two Rivers is not a world apart; its people are one strand of a larger country |
| Mat's "Moiraine... The Ferry"; Nynaeve rowed across by Hightower | Comedy | Nynaeve's will matches Moiraine's; the Wisdom who can channel has come after them |
| Min: "Run, if you want. You can't escape from me" | A crazy girl | Not a threat but a viewing: she is bound to Rand |
| Thom's grim look at the White Tower's part in the false Dragons | An old man's caution | Thom knows the Tower's ways too well, for reasons not yet told |

## Craft lessons

1. **Confirm the supernatural with a domestic detail.** A cook defending her cat delivers the proof that the dream was real. The horror lands harder for arriving in a kitchen quarrel.
2. **Hide the future in a list.** Min's viewings name half the series' set pieces in one breath. A first reader takes them as colour; a rereader finds a map.
3. **Let the point-of-view character misread his own body.** Rand calls his fever a cold, then madness. The reader, who saw Bela run, suspects more. Unreliable sensation builds dread without a word of exposition.
4. **Make a prank the trigger for danger.** Mat's sling shot is the comedy that puts Rand in front of three armed zealots.
5. **Bring back a minor character changed.** Fain the gossiping peddler returns gaunt, ragged and furtive. The change itself is the clue.
6. **Give a mentor a limit.** Thom knows the names and fears them, but cannot say whether the Tower used them. His honest "I cannot see that" makes his advice more credible.
7. **End on a comic threat.** Nynaeve inside, "cold enough in there to snow". The boys march in "as if to face Trollocs", a laugh that also raises the next chapter's stakes.
