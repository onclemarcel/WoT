Chapter 5 is where the stories come true. Tam and Rand spend a quiet afternoon on chores at the farm, Tam locks the doors and brings out a heron-marked sword, and after dark Trollocs break in. Tam holds the door, leads the pack away from Rand, and comes back wounded and burning with fever. Rand goes back alone, kills a Trolloc that waited for him, and learns that a Myrddraal wants to talk to him. The chapter turns a farm boy's fear into his first act of courage, and ends on a hope the reader already distrusts: once they reach the village, everything will be all right.

## Setting

The whole chapter happens on one farm and the woods beside it, and the same few places are shown twice: first safe at dusk, then wrecked by night. The long first half of chores is there so the second half has something to destroy.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The farmhouse | A small, tidy house with no wings, home to two men living alone in the Westwood | A door that has never been locked, and an old chest under Tam's bed |
| The main room | Fire, candles, a table for a dozen, Tam's reading chair and *The Travels of Jain Farstrider*; "no false Dragon here" | The room the Trollocs smash, dust with flour and salt, and foul beyond cleaning |
| The farmyard: barn, sheep pen, curing shed | Wool and tabac, a cow drying up, three eggs, stunted cabbages | The long winter as a slow threat: the cellar may run out before anything grows |
| The woodpile | Stacks reaching the eaves, far more than usual this late in the year | How long the cold has lasted; Rand loses himself in the axe and misses the dusk |
| The sheep pen at night | Silence where there should be bleating | The flock killed for no reason: "They kill for fun" |
| The barn | Empty stalls, doors torn off, the cart on its side | Bela and the cow gone; one broken shaft gives Rand his idea for a litter |
| The Westwood | Cover from the Trollocs, then a place where branches seem to reach for him | Tam, waiting in the dark, with a wound and a fever |

**The moon.** A full moon "bulging as if about to fall on their heads" rises over the chapter. It lights the yard just enough to make every shadow a possible Trolloc.

## Characters

Only two people are on stage, and the chapter swaps their roles: Tam protects Rand in the first half, and Rand protects Tam in the second. One Trolloc, Narg, gets a name and a voice.

| Character | Mindset | Wants | Key moment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rand | Tired, then terrified, then ashamed of running; finally steady because he must be | A hot bath and Festival; then only to save Tam | Goes back alone to the wrecked house and kills Narg, almost by accident, on Tam's blade |
| Tam | Uneasy, then calm and deadly; wounded and feverish by the end | To keep Rand alive, at any cost to himself | Leaps through a window and draws the Trollocs away, shouting "Run, lad!" to an empty forest |
| Narg, a Trolloc | Proud of being "smart"; afraid of the Myrddraal | To take the boy alive for a Fade | "Myrddraal want talk you": the first sign that the attack was about Rand |
| The Trollocs | Loud, cruel, "lazy", easy to mislead | To kill, and to find someone | They wreck the house, kill the sheep, take Bela and the cow, and are sent off toward the mountains |

**Named but not on stage:** Rand's mother, who disliked the sword and died years ago; Oren Dautry, the neighbour an hour away; Nynaeve, the Wisdom Rand must reach; Lan, whose sword Tam's resembles; Perrin, Rand's quarterstaff partner; Master Luhhan, the measure for Narg's size; the Coplins, Rand's word for a fool.

## The stories come true

Until now Trollocs and Fades were tales told by the fire. This chapter tests each tale against what Rand sees, and the result is mixed: some stories are true, some are wrong, and the true ones are worse. Tam's short briefing is the first practical knowledge anyone gives Rand about the Shadow.

| Creature | What the stories say | What Rand sees or learns |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trollocs | "Just a story"; they hate men and serve the Dark One | Taller than any man, in spiked black mail, with ram's horns, wolf muzzles, feathers or hooves on half-human bodies |
| Trolloc senses | (nothing) | They see better than men in the dark, but bright light blinds them; some track by scent or sound, some hear like a dog |
| Trolloc habits | They serve the Dark One | They kill for pleasure, are "lazy", give up a hunt, and can be trusted only when afraid, "and then not far" |
| Trolloc speech | (nothing) | A harsh tongue "unsuited to a human tongue"; Narg also speaks broken human words |
| Myrddraal (Fade) | Twenty feet tall, eyes of fire, riding shadows; vanishes when it turns sideways; walls cannot stop it | Not seen yet. Only Narg's fear of it, and the news that it wants to talk to Rand |

The stories are wrong in the details and right about the danger. A Fade is not twenty feet tall, but a Trolloc's fear of it says more than any tale.

## What the reader can't decode yet

Tam's past breaks the surface in this chapter, and almost every detail of the sword points at it. In fever he says more than he means to.

| Sign | What the village sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A heron on the scabbard, the hilt and the blade | A fine old sword | A heron-mark blade: the sign of a blademaster, a rank won by beating another blademaster |
| "Two coppers is too much for one of these" | A joke about a bad bargain | Tam is hiding what it really cost him; a heron blade is earned, not bought |
| Slightly curved, one edge, "a good deal like Lan's sword" | Rand's surprise | The same make as a Warder's sword, from the far North |
| It cuts seasoned ash like an axe and stays razor-sharp | A wonder | A blade made with the One Power in the Age of Legends; such blades do not dull |
| "I got it a long time ago, a long way from here" | Tam once travelled | Tam left the Two Rivers as a young man, fought in the wars of the wide world, and rose high as a soldier |
| His mother "had been an outlander" | Family history | Kari al'Thor came from outside; Rand has always assumed she was his birth mother |
| Tam holds a doorway and fights a dozen | A strong farmer | A trained soldier who knows how to use a narrow door and a light |
| "Dreams of days gone. Nightmares." | A feverish murmur | Tam is reliving his past; his fever talk will soon tell Rand something that unsettles him about his birth |
| Narg: "Myrddraal want talk you" | Monsters raiding a farm | The attack has a target: someone the Fade wants alive |
| The Trollocs came in force, with a Fade, to an isolated farm on Winternight | Bad luck | The Shadow knows the Two Rivers holds a boy it wants, and is hunting houses with young men of the right age |
| Tam's wound burns with fever within hours | An infected cut | Trolloc and Fade blades carry a sickness beyond ordinary medicine; Nynaeve's herbs may not be enough |

## Craft lessons

1. **Build the room before you break it.** Pages of chores, stew and firelight make the reader feel at home, so the wreck hurts. The house is described twice: whole, then smashed.
2. **Signal danger through small breaks in habit.** A locked door, a chest dragged from under a bed, a sword at a farmer's hip. Each is ordinary elsewhere and alarming here.
3. **Let the hero act before he thinks.** Rand throws the kettle "without thinking". His first blow is a reflex, which keeps him a farm boy and not a born warrior.
4. **Make the monster talk.** Narg's broken speech and his fear of the Fade turn a beast into a servant with a master, and reveal the plot: someone wants Rand.
5. **Test the legends against sight.** The stories are wrong about the details and right about the danger, so the reader learns to trust neither fully.
6. **Swap protector and protected.** Tam saves Rand, then Rand must save Tam. One reversal turns a boy into the one who has to decide.
7. **Let an object carry a secret.** The heron sword says more about Tam than any line of dialogue, and its impossible sharpness hints at a history no one explains.
8. **End on a hope the reader doubts.** "Everything's going to be all right" echoes the relief at the end of Chapter 4, and the reader trusts it even less.
