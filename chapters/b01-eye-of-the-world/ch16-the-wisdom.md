Chapter 16 sits Nynaeve and Moiraine at one table and makes the boys pick a side. Min warns Rand that the dark-braided woman is "part of it", and that the danger got sharper when she came. In the private room the cold comes from the two women. Nynaeve tracked Lan's hidden trail, blushes at his praise, and wants the four home. Rand says "We can't" first, and Moiraine answers with what saidar can protect them from. Alone in the hall, Nynaeve tells Rand about the split village meeting and the Women's Circle. Then she lets slip, by a heartbeat's pause, that he was born outside the Two Rivers.

## Setting

The whole chapter happens indoors, in two rooms of one inn, and both are places where people listen at doors.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The inn's hallway, with Min | A girl gripping Rand's arm; the others waiting, impatient | Sparks "sharper, and more furious" since Nynaeve came; Min's gift, which Rand keeps secret |
| The private dining room | A blazing hearth, a silver tray, pitcher and cups; the room where they ate the night before | A cold that comes from the two women at the table, not the weather |
| The table itself | Moiraine and Nynaeve at opposite ends; the four young people in the middle chairs | Who will decide for them, and whether they decide for themselves |
| The hall outside the shut door | Lan on guard across the corridor, the others sent out of earshot | What Moiraine and Nynaeve say alone; the reader is shut out too |
| The hallway, walking | Nynaeve taking Rand's arm, away from the still-open door | A door that may have a listener; Rand thinks he sees something move |

## The new balance of the party

Nynaeve's arrival turns a party with one leader into a party with two poles, and every other relationship shifts to fit. The seating says it first: Moiraine and Nynaeve at opposite ends, the four young people in the middle, Thom by the door, Lan at the fire. Nynaeve's own saying names the shape: "Whether the bear beats the wolf or the wolf beats the bear, the rabbit always loses."

| Relationship | Before Nynaeve (Ch. 10–15) | After her arrival (Ch. 16) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who decides for the four | Moiraine alone; the boys follow, grumbling | Two adults pull opposite ways; the village now has a voice in the room |
| Rand | Follows, keeps his doubts private | Speaks first, "We can't"; argues with the Wisdom and presses her; she tells him "You've grown" |
| Egwene | Moiraine's pupil, hair unbraided like a grown woman | Back to a girl hiding under her hood, behind Perrin |
| Mat and Perrin | Hiding the dream from Moiraine | Hiding from two women now; reduced to asking about their fathers and master |
| Lan | Moiraine's shadow; nothing surprises him | Surprised for the first time; praises Nynaeve, who blushes. A new line opens between them |
| Thom | An outsider tagging along | Takes Moiraine's side on secrecy, partly for his own neck |
| Moiraine | Unchallenged; her answers are taken as given | Must argue her case aloud, and loses her patience |
| Secrets | The boys keep secrets from Moiraine | Nynaeve keeps one from Moiraine (Rand's birth); Rand keeps one from his friends (Min) |
| Min's sparks | Needed three or four of them together | Two women alone make sparks; the danger is "sharper, and more furious" |

**What the shift does.** It gives Rand room to grow. With one leader he could only follow or sulk; between two he has to choose, and choosing is the first time he leads. The chapter also changes the kind of authority on stage. Moiraine's is power and knowledge; Nynaeve's is the village and the body (she tracks, she heals, she knew Rand as a baby). Each one has something the other lacks.

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter's biggest clue is a question Moiraine tries to disguise: was any of the boys born outside the Two Rivers?

| Sign | What the village sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Min: Nynaeve is "part of it"; sparks with just two women | A crazy girl's talk | Nynaeve's fate is tied to the boys'; often read as a sign she can channel, like Egwene |
| Nynaeve "somehow" sure they dared not wait a day | A Wisdom's stubbornness | The text never says; often read as her untaught gift, "listening to the wind" |
| Nynaeve tracks a trail Lan tried to hide | A good hunter's daughter | Her skill and will are a match for the Warder's; the first sign she will not be left behind |
| She blushes at Lan's praise | Embarrassment | The start of a feeling neither of them names yet |
| Moiraine asks if any boy was born outside the Two Rivers | An odd question | She is looking for one boy in particular, born in a certain place and time |
| Nynaeve's heartbeat pause, then "Of course" | Nothing | She knows Rand came to Emond's Field as a baby, and hides it from Moiraine |
| Tam came back with "a red-haired, outlander wife and a babe" | Old village memory | Tam's fever talk may be true; Kari loved Rand as her own, which is not the same as bearing him |
| Saidin's taint "sometimes" makes men more vulnerable | Aes Sedai lore | Seeds what Rand felt in Ch. 11 and Ch. 15 |
| Lan feels Trollocs within a quarter mile | A Warder's trick | A Warder's bond and Borderland training; the text does not yet say how |
| Rand sees something move at the open door | Nerves | Someone, perhaps Moiraine, may be listening; the text never says |
| "Today, I am going to have a bath" | She has not decided | She is staying, for now |

## Craft lessons

1. **Stage a power struggle as seating.** Two women at opposite ends of a table, the young in the middle, Thom by the door. The reader sees the balance of power before anyone speaks.
2. **Add a second authority to grow the hero.** With one leader Rand could only follow; between two he has to choose, and his "We can't" is the first time he speaks for the group.
3. **Crack a hard character once.** Nynaeve is never out of countenance, then blushes at Lan's praise. One small break makes her human and opens a thread.
4. **Let a secret slip through a pause.** Nynaeve's "Of course" comes a heartbeat late. Rand catches it because he is watching for it, and so does the reader.
5. **Shut the reader out with the boys.** The private talk between Moiraine and Nynaeve happens behind a door. The reader gets only Nynaeve's edited account, and wonders what she left out.
6. **Make a proverb carry the theme.** The bear, the wolf and the rabbit say in one line what the whole chapter is about.
7. **End small after big news.** "Today, I am going to have a bath." A plain line after the revelation about Rand's birth leaves her choice open.
