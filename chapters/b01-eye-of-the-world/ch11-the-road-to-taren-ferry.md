Chapter 11 is one long gallop, and its subject is power. The party races up the North Road to Watch Hill, where Moiraine washes the fatigue from the horses. A Draghkar strikes and marks them for the Myrddraal. Moiraine answers with a fog that hides them all the way to Taren Ferry, where Lan buys a night crossing with gold. Along the way Rand wills Bela to run, draws Tam's sword at the sky without knowing it, and learns that what Moiraine does for others she cannot do for herself.

## Setting

The chapter is a corridor: a dark road, one lit village that has not heard the news, a fog that erases everything, and a sly town at the river. The Two Rivers thins out as the party moves north, and the last stop is barely Two Rivers at all.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The North Road by night | Hard-packed dirt, watery moonlight, farmhouse lights and barking dogs that flash past | "Shadows that could hide an army"; the Draghkar above |
| Watch Hill | Windows covering a hill "like fireflies"; song and cooking smells; the White Boar inn | A village still celebrating Bel Tine, with no idea what happened in Emond's Field; it hears the scream, then plays on |
| The fog | A wall of cold gray mist that muffles hooves and swallows sky, road and horses' legs | Moiraine's work: cover from the Draghkar, and a trick played on the Myrddraal |
| Taren Ferry | Tall houses on high redstone foundations against the spring flood from the Mountains of Mist | A town with "noses up" toward the lower villages, known for slyness: count your fingers after a handshake |
| Master Hightower's door | A stair as high as their heads, a nightshirt, an oil lamp, a narrow face | The price of the crossing: gold now, as much again on the far bank |

## Power on the road

The chapter shows the One Power doing four things in one night, and each comes with a limit. It also shows two older kinds of power, gold and will, and lets them sit side by side with the magic.

| Act | Who | What the text shows | The limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bela keeps pace | Rand, or so it seems | He silently wills her to run; his skin prickles, his bones feel frozen; "And Bela ran" | Rand credits Moiraine: she "must have done something more" |
| Fatigue washed away | Moiraine | Her hands on each horse's neck; Cloud dances "as if he had spent a week in a stable" | "What she can do for others, she cannot do for herself"; she alone will ride tired |
| The Draghkar | Moiraine | "I cannot!": she will not try to kill it, least of all from a galloping horse | Some enemies cannot be fought, only outrun |
| The fog | Moiraine | Mist that rises from the ground, hides the party, and silences the Draghkar's cries | It leaves the riders damp and afraid to breathe; Lan notes there is still only one place they can be going |
| The ferry | Lan | Gold counted coin by coin into Hightower's palm, half now, half on the far bank | Gold buys a crossing, not loyalty, in a town famous for tricks |

**Rand's two blind spots.** He does not see Moiraine working among the horses until she is done, and he does not notice his own hand drawing the sword. The chapter teaches the reader to watch what Rand misses.

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter hides its biggest sign inside a sentence about a horse. The rest are limits and plans the professionals mention and do not explain.

| Sign | What the party sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rand's prickling skin, freezing bones, and "And Bela ran" | Moiraine must have helped the mare | Rand's first touch of the One Power, unknowing. The text never names it, but Moiraine, who does not lie, finds Bela "the least weary of all" before she has touched her |
| "What she can do for others, she cannot do for herself" | A Warder's remark | A rule of the Power: a channeler cannot heal herself, and channeling tires the one who does it |
| "Too tired for what?", left unanswered | Lan ignoring a question | A tired Aes Sedai cannot protect them; Moiraine is the party's only real shield |
| Lan's "I don't need it, not yet" | Toughness | Warders endure more than other men (see the Warder stories of Chapter 9) |
| "I will use its own slyness against it" | A plan for the fog | Moiraine means to mislead the Myrddraal about where they went; the next chapter shows how |
| "There is still only one place we can be going" | Lan's worry | The Fade knows the road ends at the ferry; the river is both escape and trap |
| Rand's sword drawn at the sky, unnoticed | Embarrassment | His hands are learning to fight before his mind agrees; Moiraine, Lan and Egwene all see it |
| Egwene's delight in the gallop | Excitement | Her hunger for the wider world, and for the Power that touched her mare |
| Cloud forcing between Lan and Moiraine; "Don't be so eager, sheepherder" | A headstrong horse | Rand pushed to the front, where he does not yet belong |
| Watch Hill hears the scream and plays on | A village's luck | The rest of the world does not know the Shadow is moving |

## Reader's notes

The notes settle the chapter's hidden event: the evidence that Rand touched the Power is in the text, and it comes from the one witness who cannot lie.

| Note | Reading |
| --- | --- |
| Rand touched the Power without knowing it | Agreed. The chain is complete: his will ("Run!"), the bodily signs (prickling skin, bones "ready to split open"), the result ("And Bela ran"), and a witness who confirms it |
| Moiraine says Bela "may be the least weary of all", and she cannot lie | The strongest clue. She says it as she reaches Bela, before washing the mare's fatigue away, so the stamina was not her doing. "Strange as it seems" shows she finds it odd. Two limits: "may be" is a hedge, and the no-lying rule is so far only Tam's "They don't lie, not right out" |
| Rand credits Moiraine: she "must have done something more" | His explanation contradicts her own words. The reader holds better evidence than the viewpoint character, which is the chapter's method |
| What Moiraine concludes | The text never says. She notices, says nothing more, and moves on; whether she suspects Rand here is left open |

**Craft point.** The author hides the event inside Rand's view, where he cannot read it, and plants the confirmation in the mouth of the one character the reader is told cannot lie. The clue is fair because its source is trusted.

## Craft lessons

1. **Put the viewpoint at the back.** Rand rides last, as rear guard. Every threat comes from behind him and every answer from far ahead, which keeps him anxious and the reader with him.
2. **Give magic a cost in the same breath as its wonder.** Moiraine's healing is shown, then limited at once: she cannot do it for herself. A power with a price creates suspense; a free one ends it.
3. **Hide a big event in a small sentence.** "And Bela ran" sits in a paragraph about a horse. A careful reader notices; a fast one does not, and both are rewarded later.
4. **Let the character miss what the reader sees.** Rand does not notice Moiraine at work, or his own drawn sword. The gap between his view and the reader's builds trust in the reader's attention.
5. **Use a lit village as contrast.** Watch Hill's songs and mutton pies show what the party has lost, and how little the world knows. The music stopping and starting again says it without a word of comment.
6. **Change the medium of a chase.** Open road, then sky, then fog: each stage changes what can be seen and heard, so a long gallop never repeats itself.
7. **End on a new kind of power.** After a night of magic, Lan wins the crossing with coins and a grip on a wrist. A world where gold works too feels larger and more real.
8. **Introduce a place by its reputation.** Taren Ferry arrives with one proverb about counting your fingers. The reader distrusts Hightower before he opens the door.
