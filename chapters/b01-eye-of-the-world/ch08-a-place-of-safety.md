Chapter 8 turns a sickroom into a trap closing slowly on Rand. Moiraine heals Tam, then she and Lan lay out, fact by fact, that the Trollocs came for three young men: Rand, Mat and Perrin. The only way to save the Two Rivers is for them to leave it, that night, for Tar Valon. The chapter ends with Rand fighting sleep in a chair, set on telling his father everything.

## Setting

The whole chapter stays in one upstairs room of the Winespring Inn. The world outside reaches it only through the window and through what people carry in: the Mayor's account, Lan's facts, a tray of food.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The sickroom | One bed, one chair, a bench against the wall, a fire; Tam rasping; Moiraine kneeling with her hands on his chest | No flashes, no thunder: the Power at work looks like a woman asleep |
| The window on the Green | People clearing the ruins; the blackened stump of the Spring Pole | The festival Rand looked forward to is a stump; the village he must save by leaving |
| The rest of the inn, through the door | Bran's "plenty to do downstairs"; Thom fleeing to smoke outside; Mistress al'Vere's tray of broth | The ordinary village goes on, kept out of a room where the real decisions are made |
| Emond's Field and the farms, in talk | Half the village in ashes; outlying farms that never saw a Trolloc | The pattern of the attack: the forge, the Luhhan house, the Cauthon house, the al'Thor and Aybara farms |
| Places named far off | Thakan'dar and Shayol Ghul; the Borderlands and the Blight; Shienar; Baerlon; Caemlyn; Tar Valon's "Shining Walls" | The map grows in one conversation, and the safe place is the farthest one |

## Characters

For the first time Rand faces the two strangers alone, without an adult of the village between them. The chapter is a conversation in which they do the knowing and he does the learning.

## The case for leaving

Moiraine and Lan never order Rand to go. They build the case one fact at a time, answer each objection, and let him say the conclusion himself. Every step closes one door.

| Step | Who | The fact | Rand's objection | What closes it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Moiraine | Rand's warning about the rider would have changed little: "An entire fist", over a hundred Trollocs | Guilt: could they have stopped it? | She takes the blame herself: the raven should have warned her |
| 2 | Lan | Some homes were never attacked; outlying farms barely saw a Trolloc | "Half the village is in ashes" | Most fires were set only for confusion |
| 3 | Lan | Two farms were struck, the al'Thors' and the Aybaras'; in the village, the forge, the Luhhan house and the Cauthon house | "That's crazy" | "Not crazy... Purposeful" |
| 4 | Moiraine | They came for young men of a certain age; Rand, Mat and Perrin are within weeks of one another | Ban Crawe and Lem Thane saw the rider too | Ban is ten months older, Lem eight months younger; neither house was touched |
| 5 | Moiraine | "There is something the Dark One fears" in one or all of them | "I'm a shepherd" | Lan: no one brings that many Trollocs that far just to burn a few houses |
| 6 | Moiraine | "They will be back" | She stopped them once; she can again | Only with more Aes Sedai, and many battles: Rand pictures the Two Rivers in ashes |
| 7 | Rand | "The Trollocs won't come back if I am not here" | "If they really are after me" | Lan: "Are you willing to bet your village on it?" |
| 8 | Rand | Baerlon or Caemlyn would be safe | (hope, not objection) | Lan: "Walls are a poor bar to a Halfman" |
| 9 | Moiraine | "There is a place of safety": Tar Valon, where he can learn why he is wanted | A city full of Aes Sedai; the unpaid price | He asks only how long Tam will sleep: the decision is made |

Two things keep the scene from being a lecture. Rand objects at every step, so the reader feels each door close. And the last step is a promise, not a threat: answers, in Tar Valon, "That I can promise." Rand notices the price has still not been named.

## What the reader can't decode yet

Chapter 8 explains more than any chapter so far: Trollocs, Fades, the taint, angreal, the hunt. What it leaves out is why. Every answer it gives points at a question it does not.

| Sign | What the village sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "In one of you, or all three, there is something the Dark One fears" | Moiraine's theory | One of them is the Dragon Reborn; she does not yet know which |
| Rand, Mat and Perrin born "within weeks of one another" | A coincidence of ages | Moiraine is matching the boys to a birth date she is looking for; all three will prove ta'veren |
| "There are places where the heron is a symbol of the master swordsman" | A Warder's curiosity | Tam is a blademaster; Lan already suspects the farmer is more than he seems |
| "He said it had no use... He must not always have thought so" | Lan prying | Tam's soldier past, which Rand has not let himself think about since the Westwood |
| Rand: if Tam has been out in the world, then maybe what he said in the fever... | A thought pushed away | Rand's origin: the fever talk was true |
| "I wish I knew how they did it": a hundred Trollocs, six hundred leagues south, unseen | A soldier's puzzle | A hidden road the Shadow is using, explained later in the book |
| The raven "should have warned me" | Ravens are just birds | The Shadow has eyes on the boys already, and will follow them |
| Healing needs "will and strength" in the patient; the old Aes Sedai could do far more | A modest healer | The Power has rules and costs, and much of its knowledge is lost; others will rediscover some of it |
| Lan sighs with relief when Rand asks only how long Tam will sleep | Nothing | Rand has agreed to go, without being forced |
| "It will be best if we keep all of this just among us" | Caution about the Coplins | Moiraine controls what anyone knows, the start of a pattern; the text never says whether Tam's absence suits her |
| "Not without half a dozen of my sisters" | Modesty | One Aes Sedai is strong but not enough; the Tower's numbers matter, and Moiraine came almost alone |
| "If Moiraine had told the truth about that, she probably had told the truth about the rest" | Rand's guess | Aes Sedai cannot speak a lie, though they can mislead |

## Craft lessons

1. **Heal first, then explain.** Tam's recovery comes before the bad news. The reader, like Rand, is grateful and off guard when the real subject arrives.
2. **Let the magic look like nothing.** Rand expects thunder; Moiraine just kneels and seems asleep. Undercutting the stories makes the Power feel real, and its cost (her stumbling walk) says more than any light show.
3. **Build the argument out of facts, not orders.** Lan and Moiraine give dates, ages and house names, and Rand draws the conclusion himself. A choice the hero states is stronger than one he is told.
4. **Give every door an objection.** Rand resists at each step, so the reader feels each one close instead of skimming a lecture.
5. **Use exposition as character.** Lan's lesson on Fades also shows Lan: dry, honest about fear, softening once. Information delivered by a person in a mood is a scene, not a glossary.
6. **Put the far world in the mouth of the near.** Shayol Ghul, the Blight and Tar Valon arrive in a sickroom, next to a cooling tray of broth. The map grows without the story leaving the room.
7. **Keep the promise unpaid.** Moiraine heals Tam, and Rand still waits for the price. A debt left open carries tension into the next chapter.
8. **End on a small, human goal.** After the stakes rise to the Dark One, the last line shrinks to a son trying to stay awake. The scale drop makes the reader care about the next page.
