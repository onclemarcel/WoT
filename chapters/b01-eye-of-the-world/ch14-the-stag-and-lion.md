Chapter 14 gives the party its first comfort in a week, then takes the comfort away in a dream. At the Stag and Lion they bathe, eat chicken and sleep in real beds. Lan silences every mention of Trollocs, the town news confirms a battle in Ghealdan won by a false Dragon named Logain, and Moiraine meets a short-haired girl called Min. That night Rand walks a stone corridor into a room under an impossible sky, where a man with fire for eyes calls himself Ba'alzamon. He offers wine, claims to have broken Lews Therin, Artur Hawkwing and the world, calls Rand a puppet of the White Tower, and asks the question the whole book turns on: "Are you the one?"

## Setting

The chapter moves from the warmest rooms of the book so far to the coldest place in it, and the warm rooms are where the warnings are given.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The Stag and Lion's back halls | Servers in long aprons who never slow; an inn "near full" of miners down from the mountains | How many ears there are: maids who listen at doors, guests who might side with the Whitecloaks |
| The bath chamber | A dozen copper tubs, a sloping tiled floor, cauldrons over the fires; a week of grime washed away | Ara's eye on the sword and the axe; the "trouble" he will not name |
| The hall outside the baths | Moiraine talking to a short-haired girl in a man's shirt and trousers | Who the girl is, and why Moiraine asked for her by name |
| The private dining room | Polished oak, a rug, a fire; chicken, turnips and henpeas; the first hot meal in a week | Lan's ear pressed to the door before anyone may speak |
| The room under the eaves | Three narrow, hard beds on the fourth floor, a window on the stableyard | Nothing; it is where Rand falls asleep |
| The dream corridor | Bare gray stone, a light with no source, dry splintered doors in pairs, water dripping that never gets closer | Rand's own memory: the inn, the Wisdom, his name for things slide away |
| Ba'alzamon's room | A balcony on a sky of streaming red, black and orange cloud; melted stone; a fire with no heat; a mirror that blurs only Rand | Faces screaming in the hearthstones, seen only from the corner of the eye |

## The dream of Ba'alzamon

Ba'alzamon's speech is a temptation in four moves, and each one is built to cut Rand off from the only people who can help him.

| Move | What he says or does | What it aims at | Rand's answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The gift | A gold goblet of spiced wine for a man dying of thirst | Something taken freely; the text never says what drinking would do | Sees smoke rise from the chair under the man's hand, and puts it down |
| The insult | Rand's father a stallion of the White Tower, his mother a brood mare | Rand's love for Tam, turned into doubt | Anger, and one clear moment: "Don't you talk about them!" |
| The history | Lews Therin, the Trolloc Wars, Artur Hawkwing: all his work | Despair: greater men failed, so a "toad" will too | "You are bound in Shayol Ghul"; "I have never been bound!" |
| The trap | Tell the Amyrlin of this dream, and the Tower will kill you to keep its secret | Silence: Rand must not tell Moiraine | Wakes, and wonders whether to ask her for help after all |

**The history he claims.** Ba'alzamon tells the past as a list of his own victories. It matches the tales the cards already hold, with one new twist each time: he was standing behind it.

| Era | His claim | Already in the cards |
| --- | --- | --- |
| End of the Age of Legends | He told Lews Therin to kill his wife, children and all his blood, then gave him a moment of sanity; Lews Therin called down the Power on himself and raised Dragonmount as his tomb | The prologue (Ch. 1); Thom's history (Ch. 4) |
| The Trolloc Wars, "a thousand years later" | He sent the Trollocs south for three centuries; the Second Covenant of the Ten Nations was "shattered beyond remaking" | Manetheren's fall, in a war of "nearly two centuries" (Ch. 9) |
| Artur Hawkwing | He whispered, and Aes Sedai died; the High King sent armies across the Aryth Ocean, sealing "a doom yet to come"; on his deathbed he burned his councilors and cried for Tar Valon's fall | Thom's Hawkwing (Ch. 4) |
| False Dragons | Davian, Yurian Stonebow, Guaire Amalasan, Raolin Darksbane, and now Logain: all used by the Amyrlin "until consumed" | Logain named this chapter; the others are new |

Two numbers clash: Moiraine's tale in Chapter 9 gives the Trolloc Wars nearly two centuries, Ba'alzamon gives them three. The text does not say who is wrong, and a liar has reasons to inflate his own work.

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter's biggest clue is a question the villain asks out loud, and the reader is meant to hear it louder than Rand does.

| Sign | What the party sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Are you the one?" | A nightmare's riddle | Ba'alzamon is hunting the Dragon Reborn and does not yet know which boy it is |
| "Once more we meet face-to-face" | A strange greeting | The red-cloaked figure of the Bel Tine dream (Ch. 9) and this man are one; often read as the same visitor reaching Mat and Perrin too |
| "Some call me Ba'alzamon" | The Dark One himself, Rand assumes | The name means "Heart of the Dark" in the Old Tongue; whether the speaker is the Dark One or a servant who claims his name, the text never says here |
| The mirror that blurs only Rand | A dream's oddness | Often read as a sign that Rand is hidden, or not fully present, in Ba'alzamon's world |
| The wine | A drink from a host | A gift taken freely; the text never says what it would have done |
| "Your father was chosen by the White Tower" | An insult to Tam and Kari | A lie built on a truth: Rand's birth is not what he thinks (see Ch. 5–6) |
| Davian, Yurian Stonebow, Guaire Amalasan, Raolin Darksbane | Names Rand does not know | Earlier false Dragons, set beside Logain as men "used" by the Tower |
| "Lews Therin Kinslayer" | A curse from the stories | The man Rand will learn he is the rebirth of |
| Armies across the Aryth Ocean, "a doom yet to come" | Old history | Hawkwing's lost armies will come back from over the sea |
| "The dead belong to me!" | A threat | His bargain with servants, and his claim on Rand, rest on death |
| "The ashes of many like you are scattered on the slopes of Dragonmount" | Tower cruelty | The men who could channel, gentled or killed, and the mountain where Tam found a baby (Ch. 6) |
| Moiraine asks "Is Min still here?" | A friend in town | Min reads people's futures in images; she will matter to Rand far more than this glimpse suggests |
| Lan sounds "almost as if he knew" Logain | Rand's guess | Only that Lan knows the world beyond the Two Rivers; the text gives no meeting |
| Rand reaches for the void to sleep | Tam's trick for calm | The discipline he will need to keep his mind his own |

## Craft lessons

1. **Give comfort before the blow.** A bath, a hot meal and a bed lower the reader's guard with Rand's. The nightmare lands harder because the chapter has just promised rest.
2. **Let a minor character carry the world's news.** Ara and Fitch both talk too much and stop short. Their half-sentences ("the Ghealdan kind of trouble") feel overheard, not explained.
3. **Show a rule by breaking it.** Lan's "keep your trust small" is not a lecture; Mat nearly tells a stranger about Trollocs, and the others have to drown him out in a scramble that is also comedy.
4. **Make a dream feel unlike waking.** Rand's memory slips, doors lead back to the same room, light has no source. The rules of the dream are wrong in small, precise ways.
5. **Let the villain tell the history.** Ba'alzamon recasts the tales the reader already knows as his victories. Backstory becomes menace, and the reader learns the past and fears the speaker at once.
6. **Tempt with something small.** The wine is only a drink, and refusing it is the hero's first win. A small, concrete choice tells more than a speech about resisting evil.
7. **End on a choice the hero must still make.** Rand wakes asking whether to trust an Aes Sedai. The villain has made the question harder, which was his aim.
