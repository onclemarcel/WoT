Chapter 12 shows what Moiraine's power costs other people, then offers it to Egwene. The party crosses the Taren in fog, with Lan's sword and Thom's dagger keeping the ferryman honest. On the far bank the ferry spins apart in a whirlpool the river has never had, and Moiraine calls it "an unfortunate occurrence". Then, in a cave of fallen trees Lan had prepared, she gives Egwene her first lesson in the One Power. A blue stone flickers, and the last light is Egwene's own. Rand watches the girl he loves step out of his world.

## Setting

The chapter moves from a place where nothing can be seen to a place where nothing can be heard. The fog hides the crossing and the crime; the tree cave hides the fire and the lesson.

| Place | What it shows | What it hides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Taren Ferry in fog | Houses that float on cloud, dim window glows, feet hidden in mist | Who is watching, and what the ferrymen would do unseen |
| The ferry landing and the barge | A plank "bridge that led nowhere", a high-sided barge, wrist-thick guide-ropes, six haulers walking the ropes | Hightower staring ahead into the mist as if he expects something |
| The Taren | A river none of them can see across; Mat and Perrin later call it "not half as wide" as they heard | A whirlpool where "never been a whirlpool" |
| The far bank | A line of fog running down the shore "as if drawn with a pen"; clear moonlit sky beyond | Moiraine's decoy: the river cloaked for miles, the road left clear |
| The tree cave | A tangle of flood-felled leatherleafs, a low tunnel, a fire on river stones, Lan's stored supplies | Not a glimmer of firelight escapes; a hidden camp planned in advance |

## Characters

The crossing is a show of force, and each traveller plays a part in it. The cave scene then splits the party in two: the women by the fire with the Power, the men around them, listening and pretending not to.

## The first lesson

The chapter is the series' first clear statement of how the One Power works. Moiraine teaches Egwene, but the men overhear, and the rules land differently on each listener.

| Rule | What Moiraine says | Who it lands on |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The source | The One Power comes from the True Source, which turns the Wheel of Time | Everyone: magic is part of creation, not a trick |
| Two halves | Saidin is the male half, saidar the female; they work against and with each other | Rand, who touched something in Chapter 11 |
| The taint | Saidin is fouled by the Dark One, "like water with a thin slick of rancid oil"; only saidar is safe | Rand, Mat and Perrin, who look as if they want their beds at home |
| Men who channel | They die, unless the Red Ajah finds and gentles them | Thom growls; Rand shifts; the Red Ajah hunts any man who "even dreamed" of it |
| Women who channel | Some are born to it and will touch the Source whether they want to or not; untaught, some die | Egwene, "very close to her change" |
| Wisdoms | Untrained women who survive often become their villages' Wisdoms | Nynaeve, never named |
| The old blood | "The old blood sings" in Emond's Field; Moiraine knew Egwene at once | The village's Manetheren past |
| Tools | Things do not hold the Power; even an angreal is only a tool; the stone only gives light | Egwene, who asks if the stone is magic |
| Endless supply | The Source cannot be used up, as a river is not used up by a mill wheel | The Power has no limit; the channeler does |

**The cost, from Chapter 11.** Lan explains the price of the fatigue Moiraine washed away: the horses would run at full speed until they dropped dead of an exhaustion they never felt. The party must ride slowly for days. Moiraine's "extra night's sleep" is a loan, not a gift.

## What the reader can't decode yet

The chapter's code is in what is said carefully: Moiraine's exact words about the ferry, Lan's warning about talk, and a lesson whose rules point at someone who is not its pupil.

| Sign | What the party sees | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "He will remember too much as it is... If I stand out in his thoughts" | Moiraine leaving the talking to Lan | She wants witnesses to remember a Warder, not an Aes Sedai; an Aes Sedai on the road would be news the Shadow could follow. Reader's notes give a second reading, with "he" as Rand |
| "An unfortunate occurrence" | A strange accident | Moiraine sank the ferry. Her words are true and admit nothing, the Aes Sedai way of speaking |
| "Not ten women in Tar Valon could do that unaided" | A boast | Moiraine is among the strongest Aes Sedai of her time |
| "Such talk can bring worse than the Dragon's Fang scrawled on your door" | Lan's caution | The Dragon's Fang marks a house suspected of the Dark One; fear of Darkfriends is already in ordinary villages |
| "Not here" about Darkfriends in Taren Ferry | Reassurance | Darkfriends are real, hidden among ordinary people elsewhere; the party will meet them |
| Thom growls at gentling | A gleeman's distaste | A personal grief tied to the Red Ajah, not yet explained |
| Men who channel die, unless gentled | A lesson for Egwene | Rand touched the Source in Chapter 11. The lesson's darkest rule is about the listener, not the pupil |
| "Wisdoms of their villages" | A remark about the past | Nynaeve, Emond's Field's own Wisdom, can channel and does not know it |
| "The old blood sings" | Village pride | The blood of Manetheren; the Two Rivers will produce unusual strength in the Power |
| "Perhaps even the Amyrlin Seat, one day" | Encouragement | A promise the series keeps |
| Moiraine "seemed taller, almost looming" | A trick of moonlight | Often read as a small use of the Power, or of Aes Sedai presence; the text never says |
| Lan's camp, wood "I left" still dry | Foresight | Lan scouted this route earlier, before Winternight; the escape was planned in advance |

| Note | Reading |
| --- | --- |

"Remember too much as it is" fits a stranger who has seen the fog and the party at night. Rand already knows exactly what Moiraine is, so she cannot avoid standing out in his thoughts. On the ferry he answers a question Rand asked for its own sake, about people, not danger, and says he is surprised Rand asks. Then he tells him why to stop talking. It is the first exchange where Lan teaches without being made to |

| Chapter | What Lan says to Rand | Mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 7 | "I don't know that you are worth it, sheepherder, no matter what she says" | Contempt |

## Craft lessons

1. **Teach the magic system through a scene, not a lecture.** The rules of the Power arrive as a lesson for one eager pupil, overheard by frightened listeners. Each rule gets an emotional reaction, so the exposition carries tension.
2. **Let the rules indict a character who isn't in the room.** The lesson is for Egwene, but "men who channel die" is about Rand, who touched the Source a chapter ago. The reader feels it; Rand does not.
3. **Show power through its aftermath.** The ferry is never seen breaking up from close by: two torches spin, wood cracks, a hauler says "whirlpool". Moiraine's calm afterwards does the rest.
4. **Give a strong character a precise way of speaking.** "An unfortunate occurrence" is true, cold and evasive. One phrase shows how an Aes Sedai can avoid lies and still hide the truth.
5. **Stage a show of force as theatre.** Lan's slouch, Perrin's axe, Thom's dagger trick: each character threatens in their own style, and Egwene claps as if at the festival.
6. **Pay off a cost later.** The fatigue Moiraine washed away in Chapter 11 returns as a debt: horses that would run until they died. Magic that bills you later stays believable.
7. **End on a gain that is also a loss.** Egwene's triumph is Rand's defeat. The same flicker of light means opposite things to the two people watching it.
