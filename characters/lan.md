Lan's arc is about a man who has given his life to a lost war learning to accept that he may live, and love, and lead. The uncrowned king of a dead nation becomes the general who rides out to lead his people again at the Last Battle. This card is a first version; it will deepen as the chapter cards advance.

## Starting point

He arrives in Chapter 2 almost without words, defined by how he moves and how he looks at people.

- **What the village sees:** a tall swordsman in a cloak that seems to fade into whatever lies behind it; hair long and grey at the temples, held by a leather band; a face of "stony planes and angles."
- **How he moves:** Rand "could think of nothing but a wolf."
- **How he looks:** cold blue eyes that weigh the three youths and give nothing away.
- **What Ewin guesses:** "a Warder"; Mat laughs it off; Ewin is right.
- **What he hides:** he is al'Lan Mandragoran, uncrowned king of Malkier, a Borderland swallowed by the Blight, and Moiraine's Warder.

## Book 1 so far (Chapters 4 to 18)

In ten chapters the silent swordsman becomes the party's scout, fighter, purse and, by Chapter 13, its teacher. Two loyalties drive everything he does: Moiraine, whose strength he guards more jealously than his own, and the mission, which he serves with contempt for anything that slows it. The change is in how he treats the boys, Rand above all.

**What Chapter 13 shows.**

- **The lessons are his idea.** He watches the bows, then: "Now if you all had bows... and if the Trollocs agreed not to come so close". He teaches the axe to Perrin and the sword to Rand: smooth motions, "almost a dance". Moiraine watches without expression and tells him the next evening to continue.
- **The void.** "Blank your mind... Empty it of hate or fear." When Rand recognises Tam's flame and the void, Lan gives him "an unreadable look", then goes back to "I cannot make a mud-footed villager into a blademaster in an hour".
- **Moiraine first.** He grumbles that she should not waste her strength healing him, presses bread and cheese on her, hushes the camp so she can sleep, and reports to her alone.
- **Hard on everyone.** Cutting words for anyone who lets a horse trot, Moiraine included; "Don't be a fool" to Avin's talk of the Dragon Reborn.
- **Still a scout.** He leaves the camp each night for "a last look around", and never stops checking the back trail even when Mat says they have lost the pursuit.

**His turn toward Rand, Chapters 7 to 13.** From "I don't know that you are worth it" (7) to persuasion (8), correction (10), explanation with a rebuke (11), teaching (12) and, at last, a sword lesson he chose to give (13). The Chapter 12 card's Reader's notes trace the first half of this; Chapter 13 completes it.

## Arc

| Book | Turning point | What shifts | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before 1 | Raised to avenge Malkier; bonds with Moiraine | His life belongs to a war he expects to die in | Duty to a dead kingdom leaves no room for himself |
| 1–4 | Guards the party; trains Rand with the sword; falls in love with Nynaeve and refuses her | Love opens a door he keeps shut | He believes a man promised to death can't marry |
| 5–6 | Moiraine vanishes and his bond passes to another Aes Sedai | He loses the one fixed point of his life | She gave him away to protect him |
| Middle books | Marries Nynaeve | He accepts that he may want a future | Nynaeve won't let him choose death for her |
| 12–13 | Rides for the Blight; the Malkieri rally to the golden crane | The lone avenger becomes a king with a people | Others choose to follow him, against his wish to go alone |
| 14 | Leads at the Last Battle and kills Demandred | The war he was born for ends | He fights to live, not only to avenge |

## Senses and surroundings

Lan's signature sense is the bond: he feels Moiraine's presence and condition at all times, and its loss nearly destroys him.

### Inner senses

- **The Warder's bond.** He knows where his Aes Sedai is and how she feels; she can lend him strength.
- **Stillness.** The swordsman's calm, close to Tam's void, that Rand learns from him.
- **The grief of Malkier.** A loss he carries silently, as duty rather than sorrow.

### Places

| Place | Feeling | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Malkier | A memory he never had | Swallowed by the Blight when he was a baby |
| The Borderlands | Home in all but name | Where the war against the Shadow never stops |
| The Blight | His destiny | Where he expects to die, and where he rides at the end |
| The road with Moiraine | Duty | Years of guarding her search |

### Objects

- **The fancloth cloak.** Fades into whatever lies behind it: Chapter 2's sign of a Warder.
- **His sword.** A blade forged with the Power, and the heron-mark of a master swordsman.
- **The hadori.** The braided leather band of a Malkieri man.
- **The golden crane.** Malkier's banner, which others raise for him.

## Peoples and powers

| Group or power | Relationship over time | Effect on him |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Malkieri | A scattered people; his by birth | The kingdom he refuses, then accepts |
| Warders and Aes Sedai | His bond and his calling | Gives him purpose, and someone to lose |
| Borderlanders | Comrades in the endless war | His model of honour |
| Two Rivers folk (through Nynaeve) | Family by marriage | A reason to live |

## Craft lessons

1. **Introduce a character by motion and gaze.** Lan barely speaks in Chapter 2; how he moves and looks is enough.
2. **A man devoted to death is a great love interest.** His refusal of Nynaeve creates conflict that is noble on both sides.
3. **Let others choose to follow.** His return to kingship works because the Malkieri come to him unasked.
4. **Tie a mentor to the hero's tools.** Lan teaches Rand the sword and the calm under fire.
