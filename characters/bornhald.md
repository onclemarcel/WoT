# WoT Character — Bornhald

Up: [Wheel of Time — Story Map](../story-map.md) · First appears: [EotW Ch. 15 — Strangers and Friends](../chapters/b01-eye-of-the-world/ch15-strangers-and-friends.md) · Order: [WoT — Children of the Light](../world/children-of-the-light.md) · Linked to: [WoT Character — Rand](rand.md) · [WoT Character — Perrin](perrin.md) · [WoT Character — Padan Fain](padan-fain.md)

Bornhald's arc is about a man who is certain, and who learns how much his certainty was used. The young Whitecloak lord who calls Rand a Darkfriend over a splash of mud becomes, in later books, the son who hunts Perrin for his father's death, then the witness who admits the wrong man was blamed. The name covers two men: Lord Captain Geofram Bornhald, the father, and Dain Bornhald, the son; the Bornhald on stage in Chapter 15, "not much older than Rand", is read here as Dain. This card is a first version; it will deepen as the chapter cards advance.

## Starting point

Chapter 15 gives Bornhald one scene, and in it he decides Rand is guilty before Rand has said a word of substance.

- **How he looks:** narrow-faced, not much older than Rand; breastplate and conical cap burnished like silver; a white cloak with the golden sunburst and, unlike his two men, a golden knot of rank beneath it.
- **How he walks:** the crowd parts around him and his men "as if by happenstance"; they look at people as at things "wriggled out from under a rotting log".
- **His tell:** an arrogant, unblinking look, "as if he knew something important" no one else knew; later, "the light of knowing" in his eyes.
- **How he speaks:** clipped, with a questioning "yes?": "You find something funny, yes?"
- **What he does:** accuses Rand of the barrels, notices the heron-mark sword when his man warns him, dismisses it ("He is too young"), and demands an answer.
- **What stops him:** not Rand but the Town Watch, a dozen men with quarterstaffs. He holds back his angrier man and leaves.
- **What he promises:** "Darkfriends do not escape us, youngling... We will meet again. You may be sure of it!"
- **What he hides (from later books):** a father in command, and a grudge that will land on Perrin rather than Rand.

**Chapter 17: the gate.** Bornhald keeps his promise that night. With four men he stops the party at the Caemlyn Gate, tells the Watch they are Darkfriends, and offers to take them to camp instead of reporting the Watchmen to the Questioners. When Moiraine looms, he alone attacks: "Aes Sedai! Die!" Her staff melts his sword almost in two and throws all five into a heap. He shouts to hold the gates; the Watch shuts them anyway. A Whitecloak spy, a scar-faced man, watched the boys at the inn earlier.

## Senses and surroundings

Bornhald's signature sense is certainty: the "light of knowing" in his eyes. He reads people by what he already believes about them, and the series makes that his weakness.

### Inner senses

- **The knowing look.** In Chapter 15 he looks at Rand as if he holds a secret; what he holds is a conclusion.
- **Contempt.** Townsfolk are things from under a log; a mud-splashed cloak is an attack on the Light.
- **Restraint.** He is the one who stops the half-drawn sword when the Watch arrives: proud, but not reckless.
- **Grief turned to purpose.** In later books his father's death becomes the only thing he feels.

### Places

| Place | Feeling | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Baerlon's streets | Superiority | A town that "stands in the Shadow" in his eyes |
| The Two Rivers (Book 4) | Righteous occupation | Where he hunts Perrin, and where Ordeith kills in his name |
| Galad's camp (Book 13) | Reckoning | Where he tells the truth about the Aybara killings |

### Objects

- **The white cloak and sunburst.** Spotless until Mat's barrels: a pride that mud can wound.
- **The golden knot.** His rank, and the reason the others wait for his word.
- **The burnished breastplate.** Armour polished like silver, for a war on people who carry no weapons.

## Craft lessons

1. **Introduce an antagonist through a verbal tic.** The clipped "yes?" marks Bornhald in a few lines and makes him recognisable when he returns.
2. **Let a small insult start a long feud.** A splash of mud and a laugh are all it takes; the reader believes the grudge because the cause is so petty.
3. **Show the institution's limits in the street.** A dozen watchmen with quarterstaffs stop three armed zealots, which tells the reader exactly how far the Children's power reaches in Andor.
4. **Make the villain's certainty his weakness.** The man who "knows" Rand is a Darkfriend cannot see the real one riding beside him in Book 4.
5. **Pay off a minor character with a confession.** His honest testimony in Book 13 closes a wrong that began in Book 1 and turns an antagonist into a witness.
