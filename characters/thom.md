Thom's arc is about a man hiding from the world learning to step back into it. The disgraced court bard who makes "a point of never knowing anything about Aes Sedai" becomes the boys' teacher of the world, a player in the Game of Houses again, and finally the husband of the Aes Sedai he distrusted on sight. This card is a first version; it will deepen as the chapter cards advance.

## Starting point

Chapter 4 shows Thom almost entirely from outside, through a performance, and every boast hides something true.

- **Who he says he is:** "Once a Court bard, I am now indeed risen to the exalted rank of Master Gleeman": a joke that admits a fall.
- **How he looks:** lean, stooped, spry; snowy hair and mustaches; a face "gnarled like a tree that had seen hard times"; piercing blue eyes; a cloak of a hundred coloured patches.
- **How he sounds:** a voice that fills a great room even in the open air, and drops to scorn when he names Fain.
- **What he wants:** an audience, a warm bed, a strong brandy, and distance from Aes Sedai and wars ("fools kill other fools for foolish causes").
- **What he hides:** why a court bard ended up in a backwater; why he stops juggling the moment Moiraine appears; how he knows that a boy's height is "as tall as an Aielman" (and why he covers it by calling Perrin "almost the size of an Ogier").
- **Why he is here:** the Council hired him for Festival, and a fallen court bard is safest in a backwater. He arrives just as an Aes Sedai, a Darkfriend peddler and a Myrddraal close on three boys; the Pattern pulls people toward ta'veren, and his later motive, saving a young man from Aes Sedai as he could not save Owyn, grows from here.
- **What he already knows:** he knows Fain by sight and by reputation, and brings Baerlon's news. His ironic lines ("You lads are widely traveled, then, it seems") show a man who has seen far more than he says.
- **His stance:** prickly and vain, but quick to apologise when Egwene calls out his mockery.

## Book 1 so far (Chapters 7 to 15)

The gleeman who wanted nothing to do with Aes Sedai ends Chapter 13 riding in one's party, healed by her against his will. He joins uninvited, keeps his distrust, and slowly finds a role: the one adult who explains the world to the boys instead of steering them. His past shows only at the edges: what he knows, what he will not say, and what makes him growl.

**What Chapter 13 shows.**

- **Healed against his will.** He jerks away from Moiraine's hands; she seizes his grey head "with a look that allowed no nonsense", and smiles mockingly when he scowls through it, refreshed.
- **Stories chosen with care.** Lan bans his harp and flute, so he juggles and tells tales: Mara and the Three Foolish Kings, Anla the Wise Counselor, the Great Hunt of the Horn, "always with a happy ending and a joyous homecoming", for boys who may not go home.
- **The teacher.** Juggling lessons each night; Mat reaches four stones. On Baerlon's streets he answers Rand about Tear, the Stone and the Sword That Cannot Be Touched, glancing first to make sure Moiraine cannot hear.
- **Prophecy, unwanted.** "May the Stone stand till I am dust": he knows the Karaethon Cycle and wants no part of its coming true. "You ask an awful lot of questions, boy."
- **Defending the boys.** "The lads were just-": Moiraine talks right over him. After her threat he cannot sleep either.
- **A past in Baerlon.** Avin remembers letting him through "some days back" and teases him about his tricks downcountry. He came south through the same gate, alone.
- **The city sneer.** Mat breathes "So that's a city"; Thom snorts "City!" A man who has seen real ones.

## Senses and surroundings

Thom's signature sense is sound: his voice, his harp and his flute. He reads people the way a performer reads a crowd, counting them, gauging his effect, noticing who has just joined.

### Inner senses

- **The audience.** He sizes up every room; in Chapter 4 he "appeared to be counting" the onlookers before giving a sample.
- **Danger.** An old courtier's nose for intrigue, sharpened by years of hiding.
- **Grief.** Owyn and later Dena, carried under the jokes and the brandy.

### Places

| Place | Feeling | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caemlyn's court | Shame and longing | Where he was great, and why he left |
| Emond's Field | Contempt, then attachment | A backwater gig that changes his life |
| Whitebridge | Sacrifice | Where he stands against a Myrddraal for the boys |
| Cairhien | Old skill, new grief | The Game of Houses, and Dena's death |
| The Tower of Ghenjei | Dread and resolve | Where he wins Moiraine back |
| Shayol Ghul | Duty | Where the storyteller becomes a guardian |

### Objects

- **The patched cloak.** A gleeman's sign; from far off a rag, up close thick and warm: a disguise that is also armour.
- **Harp and flute.** His art in two cases; Rand will earn his bread with the flute.
- **The knives.** A juggler's tools that are also weapons, hidden in his sleeves.
- **The pipe.** It vanishes "somewhere inside his cloak": sleight of hand as a way of life.

| Aes Sedai | Hatred after Owyn; wary respect; marriage to Moiraine | The grudge that defines him, and the one he overcomes |

| Cairhienin nobility (the Game of Houses) | A game he plays too well | Costs him Dena, and makes him a regicide |
| Myrddraal and the Shadow | He fights them for the boys | Proves the cynic is brave |

| Gleemen and bards | His craft and his cover | Lets him go anywhere and hear everything |

## Craft lessons

1. **Make the cynic the one who cares.** Thom's "never knowing anything about Aes Sedai" sets up every risk he later takes.
2. **Drip the backstory.** The court bard, Owyn, Morgase: each comes out years apart, so he keeps surprising.
3. **Give the mentor a craft the hero can carry.** Thom's flute keeps Rand fed after Thom is gone.
4. **Let the storyteller enter his own tales.** Noal turning out to be Jain Farstrider closes a loop opened in Chapter 4's list of stories.
5. **Turn first antipathy into the arc's end.** His glare after Moiraine in Chapter 4 becomes their marriage.
