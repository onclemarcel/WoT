# WoT Character — Faile

Oct 12, 2026 · @OncleMarcel

Up: [Wheel of Time — Story Map](../story-map.md) · Character: [WoT Character — Perrin](perrin.md)

Faile is the person who chooses Perrin as he is and then refuses to let him stay small. She pushes him to lead, anchors him against the wolf, and her capture triggers his fall in Books 9–11. She is a leader in her own right, not only his wife.

## Starting point

Faile enters in Book 3 as a Hunter for the Horn calling herself Faile ("falcon" in the Old Tongue). She hides that she is Zarine Bashere, daughter of Davram Bashere and kin to Saldaea's queen, because she ran from the duties of her rank.

- **What she believes:** a life worth living is an adventure, and a person proves their worth by choosing their own road.
- **What she wants:** a story of her own, away from the court; then, once she meets him, Perrin.
- **Her hidden fear:** being kept, managed or set aside, whether by her family or by Perrin.
- **How she enters:** she attaches herself to Perrin, Moiraine, Lan and Loial and refuses to be sent away. Perrin's attempts to protect her by pushing her off are the first friction between them.

Min's viewing of a hawk and a falcon around Perrin (Book 1 or 2) foreshadows her, and her name makes the link explicit.

## Arc

Her arc runs from runaway adventurer to a lady who leads beside Perrin, with captivity as her own test of endurance. The Why column carries the lesson.

| Book | Turning point | What shifts | Why |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | Joins Perrin's group; rescued from a trap in the Stone of Tear | She chooses him, against his wish to send her away | He sees her as someone to protect; she wants to be a partner |
| 4 | Goes to the Two Rivers; marries Perrin; rallies the villages | She becomes Lady of the Two Rivers, and his co-leader | She steers him into leading and does the political work he avoids |
| 5–7 | Cairhien and Rand's camps; jealousy of Berelain; builds Cha Faile, her own band of young nobles | She runs her own network of eyes and ears | Saldaean custom expects a strong husband and an active wife; Perrin misreads both |
| 8–11 | Captured by the Shaido; made gai'shain under Sevanna; plots her own escape | She endures, keeps her dignity, and acts rather than waits | Captivity tests her as Perrin's search tests him |

| 13–14 | Leads alongside Perrin; carries the Horn of Valere toward the Last Battle | She becomes a guardian of one of the war's keys | Her arc ends where it began, with the Horn, but now as duty, not adventure |

## With Perrin

Faile is Perrin's main pusher and his anchor; when she is gone, he loses both.

| She gives him | She costs him |
| --- | --- |
| A reason to lead: she wants him to claim his place | Constant friction he cannot read, even though he can smell her moods |
| An anchor against the wolf: at the end it is his love for her that holds against Lanfear | Jealousy and tests, especially around Berelain |
| A political partner who handles nobles and courts | When she is taken, his whole world narrows to getting her back |

The friction is mostly cultural. Two Rivers men are taught to be gentle and quiet with women; a Saldaean wife expects her husband to shout back. Perrin's patience reads to her as weakness, and her temper reads to him as attack. Learning to argue with her is part of learning to lead. How she reaches him through his sense of smell is in [WoT Character — Perrin](perrin.md) (Senses and surroundings).

## Senses and surroundings

Faile is a Borderlander: she grew up within reach of the Blight, and it shows in how she reads a place. She looks for exits, carries hidden knives, and treats every court as a field of battle.

### Through Perrin's nose

For most of the series the reader meets her through Perrin's point of view, and so through his sense of smell. Her moods arrive as scents before she speaks: jealousy sharp and prickly, anger hot, affection warm. He reads what she feels perfectly and misreads why, which is the whole of their early friction in one device. Her own point-of-view chapters come later, mainly during her captivity, and give the reader her side at last.

### Places

| Place | Feeling | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Saldaea | Home she ran from | A hard Borderland court: rank, duty and expectations she escaped by becoming a Hunter |
| The open road | Free | The life of a Hunter for the Horn: adventure, a story of her own |
| The Stone of Tear | Trapped | Caught in a Forsaken's trap and rescued by Perrin: the first time she needs him |
| The Two Rivers | Hers to win | She arrives an outsider and leaves its Lady, rallying villages that had never had one |
| Cairhien and the courts | At home | Intrigue suits her; she builds Cha Faile there |
| The Shaido camp | Captive | White gai'shain robes, cold, labour, humiliation; she survives by learning the camp's rules and using them |
| The Ways | Danger with purpose | Near the end she carries the Horn of Valere through the Ways, the duty her old adventure became |

### Objects

- **Her knives.** Hidden, always at hand: the Borderlander's habit of never being unarmed.
- **The white robes.** The gai'shain garment is the sign of her lowest point and of the patience she learns inside it.
- **The Horn of Valere.** She began as a Hunter for it and ends as its guardian, which closes her arc.

## Peoples and powers

Each people Faile deals with tests a different strength: her pride, her cunning, or her endurance.

### Groups

### History

- **The Borderlands.** Saldaea has guarded the Blight for generations; Faile's toughness is inherited duty, even when she is running from it.
- **The Horn of Valere.** The legend that sent her hunting frames her whole arc: she meets Perrin as a Hunter and ends as the Horn's keeper.

### Craft notes on the setting

1. **Show a character through another's sense.** Perrin's nose lets the reader know Faile's feelings long before her own chapters.
2. **Give a background that explains a habit.** The Borderlands explain her knives, her pride and her readiness to fight.
3. **Let a place test the character.** Captivity strips her of rank and leaves only what she really is.
4. **Close the arc on an object.** The Horn she hunted becomes the Horn she guards.

## Craft lessons

1. **A love interest with her own arc.** Faile wants things that have nothing to do with Perrin, so she can push against him and not just reward him.
2. **Friction from culture, not malice.** Their fights come from clashing customs both of them think are normal, which keeps both sympathetic.
3. **Absence as pressure.** Taking her away removes Perrin's anchor and shows what he becomes without it.
4. **Parallel trials.** Her captivity and his search run side by side, so each tests the other's values from a different side.
5. **A name as foreshadowing.** "Faile" means falcon, so a viewing about a falcon pays off the moment she gives her name.
